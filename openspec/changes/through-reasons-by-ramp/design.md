## Project-lead request

The project lead, about the Assumptions tab and its "Why cars cut through" reasons: "can you group by the specific on ramp? These two groups are pretty similar. > Southbound Telegraph Ave and west-side traffic turning off Alcatraz Ave onto Colby St or Hillegass Ave to reach the SR-24 on-ramps > Berkeley and College Ave traffic using Hillegass Ave, Colby St and nearby side streets to reach the SR-24 on-ramps. You can do something similar for traffic coming from the specific off ramps."

## Decisions

1. **Ramps are data derived from the network, in one place.** `deriveRamps` (`tools/lib/ramps.ts`) is the single definition. An on-ramp is a chain of freeway-class links whose first link starts at a surface node and which runs on through nodes that touch no surface street and have one freeway link in and one out, until it merges into the freeway or reaches a gate. An off-ramp is the mirror image. A chain from one surface street to another is a connector, not a ramp. Chained links are one ramp. The earlier ramp-choice code treated chained links as separate ramps; the calibration work should import `deriveRamps` instead of a second definition. Ids are the kind and the OSM id of the surface node, so they are stable for a network. Labels come from the freeway direction where the ramp joins or leaves it and the surface streets at its terminal (busiest class first); the area config can override a label (`throughIntents.ramps.labels`) and names the freeway (`freewayName`). Nothing area specific is in code.
2. **The builder writes the ramps; the simulator reads them.** The demand file gets `ramps` (id, kind, node, links, label, direction) beside `intents`. An intent may carry `onRamp` or `offRamp` (a ramp id or `other`) and a `section`; the builder tags the per-flow `intent` only for reasons that name no ramp. The simulator never derives ramps itself, so builder and simulator cannot disagree.
3. **Classification happens at the moment a through car is counted**, from the car's actual path: an off-ramp is a ramp link in the links the car has driven (for a car placed at start-up, in the route before its position); an on-ramp is the first on-ramp link on its planned route ahead. The first intent that fits wins, in config order; `other` takes a ramp no listed intent names. A car that later re-plans to another on-ramp stays where it was counted (accepted approximation). The simulator counts how often that happens (`on_ramp_plan_check`); over one PM hour at seed 1, 0 of the 170 counted on-ramp cars that completed their trip drove a different ramp. The per-reason link usage, trip-end tally and totals use the new slot.
4. **Where cars start and end is computed from the run.** The simulator tallies each reason's cars by origin and destination node (`intent_trips`); `tools/lib/intent-ends.ts` turns that into approach-group shares; `precompute-results` writes them into `baseline-intents.json`; the card says "In the model these cars mostly start in ..." (on-ramp reasons) or "are bound for ..." (off-ramp reasons). No number in a note is written by hand.
5. **Config.** `throughIntents` has `sections`, `ramps` and intents in three sections. A ramp reason is listed when the ramp carries about 3% or more of the PM peak's through cars (seed 1): the Claremont Ave on-ramp at Hudson St (28%) and the SR-24 eastbound off-ramp at Claremont Ave / Clifton St (21%). The rest fall under Other on-ramps (1.6%) and Other off-ramps (2.3%). Basis and notes say which count limits each reason and what is assumed.
6. **UI.** Section headings in the results panel and the Assumptions tab. A ramp reason draws a labeled point at the ramp's surface end on the live map and on the card's map, besides the path. The marker is a diamond with a text label, so color is not the only signal.
