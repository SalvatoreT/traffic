## Context

PM peak, ten seeds, scale 1.0. Before: network c05a22fc9ebe8816, demand 259ceb1f64135196 (round 20). The model's purpose is through-car numbers on the residential streets of the study area, Hillegass Ave and Colby St most.

## Decisions

D1. Owner decision, 2026-11-14. Asked whether to force the five counted Alcatraz Ave turn-ins the way round 20 forced the Colby St northbound left, the owner answered instead: "Can you try mapping out intent for cut through like Southbound Telegraph cutting through Colby to get to 24 Eastbound. Or Berkeley traffic bypassing College via Hillegass to get to 24, etc." So through cars are explained and shaped by intent (where the driver comes from, where they are going, which arterial they avoid), not by spreading assumed trips over the seed table by volume. Part A is a diagnostic that ships as a report; part B shapes demand by intent.

D2. Approach groups are data in the area config. A rule matches a gate by street names, road classes, entry or exit role and the compass bearing from the study area's center; the first match wins; overrides fix failures; sets name unions. A node that is not a gate (a census-block loading node) takes the group of the nearest surface gate by straight line (ASSUMPTION: a coarse place name; the block's real approach is not known). 20 groups cover the 123 gates of the Rockridge model; the freeway gates are split by direction and end ("SR-24 eastbound on-ramps" is the exit gate toward the Caldecott Tunnel, "SR-24 eastbound off-ramps" the entry gate from Oakland), because the model's freeway is its two ends and the ramps are routes to them. The derivation is in TypeScript and the result goes to a small JSON file that the Rust report reads, so there is one implementation.

D3. The intent map reads the select-link trips of the calibrate run. "Through" is the simulator's own flag (neither trip end at an `in_study_area` node). The study-area total adds every residential and service link inside the study area to the select links, so it counts through cars that use any of them (cut-through). The arterial a trip would otherwise use is the shortest free-flow path (length over speed limit, no turn costs) with those links closed, labeled with the boundary streets it runs on for at least 100 m within 400 m of the study area (ASSUMPTION: the thresholds). It ignores signal delay and turn bans, so it says which arterial is avoided, not what the driver would have saved.

D4. What the data imply about intent. No source gives origins and destinations. The volunteer count gives, per block, the cars that entered at one end, the whole-street cars, the cars whose trip ended along the street, the turn-ins and turn-outs at Alcatraz Ave and the south end, and the headline freeway shares. The freeway-bound (or freeway-origin) cars that did not join mid-street are the block's freeway trips times entering / (entering + joined mid-street) (ASSUMPTION: cars that join on the way are as likely to be freeway trips as those that entered). Applied to the turn-ins in proportion: 0.50 of the Hillegass Ave turn-ins (87 of 174 veh/h), 0.365 of the Colby St turn-ins (46 of 126), 0.40 of the Colby St northbound exits (65 of 164) and 0.37 of the Hillegass Ave northbound exits (40 of 109). Ranges: low is the whole-street share (or the observed freeway share times the whole-street share), high the unadjusted freeway share. Which side cars come from (Telegraph Ave side, College Ave side) is observed through the count's direction of the turn; which group within a side is an assumption except where a via share is observed (22% of Colby St southbound trips came by way of Telegraph Ave). Only 4 of the 182 cars entering Colby St at the south end came from the northeast of Claremont Ave, so no through traffic from the hills is placed on the northbound side.

D5. Intents on assumed movements. `intents` replaces the round 20 through share: the candidates of an intent are the candidate flows (detour limit 1.3 times the best route, no loop, as D3 of round 20) whose origin group and destination group are the intent's. Volume is a share of the counted movement, or PM veh/h scaled with the period's count; the intents of one movement may not exceed it. The remainder of a count is left to the natural flows, which the fit scales as before. Local intents use the reserved group `study-area` (the study area's own nodes).

D6. Double counting. The plain flow of each pair gives up the trips its assumed flow carries (at most 80% of it, over all movements together) before the fit. The fit then rescales every plain flow to the counts within its bounds (0.1 to 3 times), so where an arterial count constrains the pair, the plain flow comes back up to what the count supports. The cut is therefore a better starting point, not a guarantee, and the build reports the volume taken.

D7. Colby St northbound left, re-expressed. 131 veh/h and the 0.62 through share kept: 0.40 SR-24 off-ramp traffic to the west side of Alcatraz Ave, 0.22 Telegraph Ave and south-side traffic, 0.38 local residents leaving. Round 20's candidates included Tunnel Rd gates (hills) that the count does not support (4 veh/h enter Colby St from the northeast); intents remove them.

D8. Movements added: the Colby St northbound right turn onto Alcatraz Ave, both Hillegass Ave northbound turn-outs, the four southbound turn-ins from Alcatraz Ave (Hillegass Ave from the west and east, Colby St from the west and east). Dana St is left out (not a counted block of the headline). All the volumes are ASSUMPTION shares of counted movements. AM and midday take the same shares (as in round 20).

D9. Stability. If the PM, AM or midday removals per hour reach 1.0 (D16) the volumes are backed off until D16 holds, and the record says by how much.
