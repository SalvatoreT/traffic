## Context

PM peak, ten seeds, scale 1.0. Before: network c05a22fc9ebe8816, demand 9971e748dcad8069 (round 19). The model's purpose is cut-through traffic on Hillegass Ave and Colby St, so through cars on those blocks matter most.

## Decisions

D1. Owner decision, 2026-11-14: force the count of the Colby St northbound left turn onto Alcatraz Ave westbound (131 veh/h PM) by adding an assumed set of trips that make that movement. It is an ASSUMPTION in code, config, the record, the demand README and the demand provenance (the Sources page lists `provenance` from the demand file). Rounds 17 to 19 showed that no data in the repository can place the trips: the router costs the movement at 19.9 s and 87% of the flows that could use it stay at the lower bound, so the fit cannot create flow on it.

D2. Mechanism, area-independent. `assumedMovementFlows` in the area config lists movements. Each is a movement of the volunteer count file, found by junction, approach and exit text, and its volume per period is the station target of that movement. Candidates are the flows of the seed table whose route through the movement costs at most `maxDetour` times their best route at free flow (the cost the simulator router uses), without a loop. The volume is split over them in proportion to their seed volume. This keeps the assumed flows to pairs the trip table already holds, so the gate and block totals the fit works from change little. Alternatives not taken: new pairs from a gravity rule (invents origins no source supports), carving the volume out of existing flows (ties the movement to flows the fit later rescales).

D3. `maxDetour` 1.3 (ASSUMPTION). In round 17 the route over Colby St cost 13% more than the one by Telegraph Ave, and each driver's perceived-cost noise is 8% per link, so a pair within 30% is one whose drivers could pick either. The median detour of the chosen pairs is 1.20.

D4. Through share 0.62 (ASSUMPTION from DATA). The volunteer count has 102 cars driving the whole of Colby St northbound and 164 leaving it at Alcatraz Ave, so 0.62 of the exits are whole-street cars. The left-turners are taken to have the same mix. Result, PM peak: 81 veh/h through (neither end in the study area), 50 veh/h local. Midday and AM take the same share.

D5. The simulator honors the movement (`via` on the demand flow). Trips are routed to the entry link, through the movement and on to the destination, and re-plans keep the movement until it is made. A fixed route was rejected: it would ignore closures and interventions, and the app lets people close links. When the movement or a link is closed or banned the trip takes its plain route. Voluntary route changes of guided drivers wait until the movement is made, so the movement does not depend on route noise. The fit's assignment routes `via` flows the same way and holds their volume, so the other flows are fitted to the counts less the assumed trips.

D6. Alcatraz Ave entries (136 against 360 counted). In the fit the three entry links are blocked at the upper bounds of their flows. The flows that cross them are local-end trips of nodes north of Alcatraz Ave, and they are small because the study-area trip-rate factor (4.6 and 6.2, from the volunteer count) applies only to blocks inside the study area. Before the side rule the same blocks loaded the boundary corners, which are study-area nodes, and took the factor by accident; that was the 325. Of the 360 counted entries about 340 are turns from Alcatraz Ave itself (76 and 50 onto Colby St, 76 and 98 onto Hillegass Ave, about 40 onto Dana St) and about 20 come straight from the streets north of it, so most of the count is traffic on the arterial that turns into the neighborhood, and the origins sit at the Telegraph Ave, Alcatraz Ave and College Ave gates and the blocks around them. Fix: blocks whose loading node lies outside the study area within `neighborM` of it take `neighborScale` times the study-area factor (ASSUMPTION: the neighborhood across the boundary street has the same unobserved non-work trips). Tested, PM, ten seeds: 400 m x 1.0 gave entries 244 and removals 3.0 per hour, 400 m x 0.5 gave 190 and 1.2, 250 m x 1.0 gave 189 and 0.0. 250 m x 1.0 kept. The count is not reached (360); what remains is the supply of Alcatraz Ave through traffic that turns in, which the router sends by Telegraph Ave and Claremont Ave.


D7. The first version of the candidate rule chose pairs whose route through the movement loops (a trip from Ward St north around to Colby St and back). The candidate test now rejects routes that come back to a node of their first leg.

D8. Not shipped, needs an owner decision. The same mechanism can force the five counted turn-ins from Alcatraz Ave (they are about 340 of the 360 entries): 76 and 50 veh/h onto Colby St from the west and east, 76 and 98 onto Hillegass Ave, 37 onto Dana St. Entries in `assumedMovementFlows.movements`, each with `at`, `from` and `to` as the volunteer count words them and a through share taken from the whole-street counts (Colby St 0.28, Hillegass Ave 0.44, Dana St the 0.45 extended through share of round 19), for example `{"id": "alc-w-hill-sb", "count": {"at": ["Hillegass Avenue", "Alcatraz Avenue"], "from": "Alcatraz Avenue from west", "to": "Hillegass Avenue toward south"}, "throughShare": 0.44, "maxDetour": 1.3, "minFlowVehPerHour": 0.5, "countShare": 1}`. PM, ten seeds, without the neighbor factor: entries 365, through cars Colby St NB 130, Hillegass Ave SB 91, Hillegass Ave NB 42, Colby St SB 101 (three of four blocks inside the observed range, against two), removals 0.20, headline error 9.6 (Hillegass Ave SB 52.4 against 68). With the neighbor factor: entries 343, removals 1.10. It adds 337 veh/h of assumed trips to the 131 of D1, which is more than the owner decided.

D9. The ramp split and the cost loop. The damped loop (`tools/cost-loop.ts --objective ramp`, successive averages of the measured link delays of ten PM seeds) ran two iterations and converged on its cost vector (gap 0.078). Both iterations made the PM peak unstable (7.9 and 6.9 removals per hour against 0.00), moved the headline error from 9.2 to about 15 points and moved the off-ramps away from their counts (ramp count error 247 to 352 and 307 veh/h). The keep rule needs removals under 1.0, so nothing is kept and no delays file ships. Free-flow costs (BPR alpha 0) and alpha 0.05 gave the same off-ramp split and 1.0 removals per hour.

D10. Colby St SB freeway share. The static assignment loads the block with 216 veh/h of long-distance pairs with no freeway end that the simulator sends elsewhere (46 veh/h), so its share (44.2%) sits below the simulator's (69.0%); the freeway-bound trips on the block are close in the two (180 and 129 veh/h). With the neighbor factor both are about 39%. No routing change was made.
