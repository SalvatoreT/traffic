## Context

Round 30 asks who the excess through cars on Hillegass Ave and Colby St southbound, 62nd St eastbound and Forest St eastbound are, and what makes the side street win for them, before anything is changed. Then it repairs the junctions that produce gridlock removals at their source, refits the demand on delays from the repaired engine, and evaluates. The order of `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md` applies. All numbers are in round 30 of the record; this file records the decisions.

## Decisions

D1. Diagnose on the router's own costs. `calibrate --od-costs` keeps a copy of the router's link and movement costs once a minute. For every through trip on the counted blocks (and on links 2622 and 2697, the counted 62nd St and Forest St eastbound blocks) it costs the surface part of the route the trip drove and the cheapest route with the study area's residential and service streets closed, on the costs in force when the trip asked to depart, split into measured link and movement time, movement traversal time, turn penalty, control constant and local-street weight. It also finds the cheapest unrestricted route at entry, so a trip can be classed as making an assumed movement (more than half of its origin-destination flow carries one), driving the router's best route, or driving a route the router ranks costlier. A copy of the router (same tables, same walk, no noise) reproduces the driven route for about 90% of the trips without an assumed movement when the route noise is off, which checks the method. PM seeds 1 to 4, on the repaired (`physical`) and the `historical` junction policy, round 29 network and demand.

D2. Repair: trip timings were not reset at time zero. `reset_results` cleared the trip records at the end of the settle interval but not the trip timings, so timings of trips completed during the settle interval stayed in the list and the two lists no longer matched by position. The first pass of D1 was wrong for that reason (trips appeared to cross 2 km in 4 s). Both lists are now cleared; the diagnostic matches by trip id; a test covers it. No run result changes (the timings feed only tests and the diagnostic).

D3. What makes the side streets win (the round's key finding). On Hillegass Ave southbound, 83 of the 177 through cars an hour make an assumed movement (the Alcatraz Ave turn-ins and on-ramp choices of the volunteer count, which imply 87), 13 drive the router's best route, and 82 drive a route the router ranked costlier than its best one at departure (median 62 s or 10% more). On Colby St southbound the split is 26 / 8 / 134 (median 74 s, 12%), on 62nd St eastbound 7 / 12 / 77 and on Forest St eastbound 6 / 42 / 133. Under the historical engine the costlier class is 18, 51, 22 and 60 an hour. So the excess is mostly trips the router's own costs did not send there: they are the perceived-cost noise of the route walk, which multiplies the whole remaining cost by a per-driver factor of up to 8% (`ROUTE_NOISE`, ASSUMPTION) at every node it passes, so a long trip meets one chance per intersection to turn off a cheaper arterial. Those chances are many on the side-street grid and the margin is small: on simulated times the side-street route and the arterial alternative of the largest group (Berkeley side streets north of Alcatraz Ave to south-east Oakland) take 477 and 470 s. For the trips that do take the best route, the arterial loses on measured time (41 to 117 s more), mostly at the College Ave southbound approach to College Ave / Claremont Ave / 62nd St (20 to 29 s per trip), Claremont Ave at Colby St / Forest St, Telegraph Ave southbound at 52nd St, 56th St and Aileen St, and Alcatraz Ave eastbound at College Ave; the local-street weight (18 to 37 s) and the control constants do not offset it. The growth from the historical to the repaired engine sits in two groups: Berkeley side streets north of Alcatraz Ave to south-east Oakland (Colby St southbound 55 against 15 an hour, Forest St 58 against 12, Hillegass Ave 30 against 9) and Ashby Ave from the west to College Ave north by way of 62nd St (34 against 3).

D4. Route noise not changed in this round. Sensitivity on PM seeds 1 to 4 (Hillegass Ave SB / Colby St SB through cars, removals per hour): noise 0: 213 / 179, 9.8; 0.04: 186 / 170, 4.5; 0.08 (as shipped): 204 / 193, 4.3; 0.16: 264 / 308, 23.8. Without noise every driver takes the same route, Telegraph Ave southbound slows to 450 s and the side streets become the best route outright, so the magnitude alone is not the lever. The form is the issue: the walk perturbs the whole remaining cost at each node, while the en-route switch rule (`path_cost`) perturbs each link's own cost. A dispersion model with a source (perturbed link costs drawn once per driver, as in probit route choice) is a change of model behavior with its own cost in run time and needs its own change and a verified reference; none is in `docs/references.md` yet.

D5. Removal hot spots, checked against OSM. The ten-seed PM removals of round 29 came from about fifteen nodes west of Telegraph Ave; the largest were 62nd St / Martin Luther King Jr Way (0.9 an hour), Adeline St / Alcatraz Ave (0.5), 60th St / Market St (0.4), 63rd St / King St (0.4) and the Harmon St circles. The seed reports show their queues come from two signals with Stanford Ave approaches: Market St / Stanford Ave and Adeline St / Martin Luther King Jr Way / Stanford Ave, where the adaptive plan gave the Stanford Ave approach 42 to 94 s of green against 15 to 33 s for Martin Luther King Jr Way and Adeline St, because the network had Stanford Ave as one lane carrying about 990 veh/h. OSM tags those one-way ways (and 51st St east of Telegraph Ave) with `turn:lanes=left||` or `||right` and no `lanes` tag; the importer took the class default of one lane. Import repair: a one-way chain whose ways carry no lane count takes its lane count from the `turn:lanes` of its last way [OSMTurnLanes]. It is applied to the finished links, because a lane count changed before chain merging renumbers links (the first version renumbered 3,123 of them), and the station ids of the frozen split are link ids. Three links change (Stanford Ave 941 and 4139, 51st St 1699), no link id changes. Not changed: the residential frontage road of Martin Luther King Jr Way at 62nd St and 63rd St and the stop where it joins the main carriageway (no OSM tag in question; its removals were spill-back from the Stanford Ave signal); the Harmon St circles (round 29 repaired circle entry). No node is exempted from removal and the 180 s hold limit is unchanged.

D6. Telegraph Ave north of 55th St, northbound. Way 416105064 (about 30 m) is tagged 3 lanes, 2 southbound (`left|`) and 1 northbound. The northbound approach to 55th St just south of it has two through lanes (`turn:lanes:backward=left||` on way 393902212) and the way north of it has 4 lanes, so one northbound lane would be a lane drop just after the signal with no room to merge: a tagging artifact. The area config gets `osmTagOverrides`, a generic way and node tag correction with a stated source, and this way is set to 4 lanes, 2 northbound, as an ASSUMPTION pending a field check. The southbound left-turn lane is kept. Measured alone (PM seeds 1 to 4) Telegraph Ave northbound fell from 364 to 355 s; the rest moved within the seed spread. It does not renumber links.

D7. Demand on the repaired engine. The network changed, so `npm run data` rebuilt the demand from the frozen split (unchanged). The delay-feedback loop (`tools/cost-loop.ts`, [PowellSheffi1982]) then ran to its own stop rule on the current engine. Its acceptance rule read the held-out GEH shares as a guard; it now uses only removals, backlog, the headline-share error and the fitted GEH shares, and reports the held-out shares. The through-car ranges are not targets in `rockridge.targets.json` (they are a check reported beside the headline shares) and were not used. Result on the merged engine (PM seeds 1 to 10 scored, AM and midday seeds 1 to 3 for the delays): the cost gap fell from 0.233 to 0.134 to 0.068, so the loop stopped converged after three iterations. Removals per hour 1.6, 7.0, 14.4 and 5.2; headline-share error 12.7, 6.1, 6.1 and 3.6 points (4 of 4 within 10 points at iterations 2 and 3); through cars on Hillegass Ave SB / Colby St SB / Hillegass Ave NB / Colby St NB 193 / 213 / 87 / 154 at iteration 0 and 134 / 174 / 77 / 131 at iteration 3, 62nd St eastbound 206 to 71 (59 counted). Every iteration has three to nine times the removals of the free-flow and BPR costs, so the rule rejects all of them and the demand stays as `npm run data` builds it. The run on the engine before the merge (7b94e620c9d8) gave the same verdict (removals 1.0, 8.2, 11.3, 8.1). As in round 28, the delays fed back move through traffic toward the observed shares by loading Telegraph Ave and the streets west of it until they jam.

D8. Lane changes along a link (case b9) and offsets at the SR-24 interchange signals on Telegraph Ave were not attempted. D3 points first at the route walk's noise and second at the College Ave / Claremont Ave / 62nd St signal, and neither engine gap has a verified source in `docs/references.md`.

D9. Engine merged during the round. The trip-tracing change (explain-traffic-redistribution) merged into main while the cost loop ran. Its uid repair changes every run (trips placed by initial loading take uids from 2^31), so the round's final figures come from the merged engine (fingerprint 169f9cf71eef). On PM seeds 1 to 4 the merged engine moved through cars and removals by less than the seed spread (removals 1.25 to 1.00 an hour, Colby St SB through cars 196 to 222). The diagnosis (D1 to D4) ran on the round 29 engine and network, as intended. The cost loop ran first on the round's engine before the merge (fingerprint 7b94e620c9d8) and was rerun on the merged engine, so the demand is judged on delays from the engine the app runs.

## Risks

- The route-noise form (D3, D4) keeps sending through trips onto costlier side-street routes; the through-car excess on Colby St southbound and Forest St eastbound stays until the dispersion model changes.
- The two lane repairs (D5, D6) rest on OSM tags, one of them on an ASSUMPTION; both need a field check.
- A network repair that changes a lane count before chain merging would renumber links and so break the frozen split; D5 applies the rule after merging, but the split's link-based station ids remain a constraint on later import repairs.
