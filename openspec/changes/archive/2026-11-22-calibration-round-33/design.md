## Context

Round 32 left one junction type between the model and an accepted delay-feedback fit: Ashby Ave / Claremont Ave, where the northbound Claremont Ave approach carried 314 of its 577 counted vehicles and the hillside streets behind it queued, and validation case l1 (a left-turn pocket that overflows every cycle) failed. The lead asked for left turns at signals to be checked as primitives first, then the phasing there, then the fit again, with each step kept only if the validation suite and the network both improve. The numbers are in round 33 of `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md`; this file records the decisions.

## Decisions

D1. Source for permissive left capacity. No entry in `docs/references.md` covered it ([HCM7] is partly verified and its chapter unread). Fambro, Gaston and Hoff (1991), TTI Research Report 0989-1F, was downloaded from the U.S. DOT repository and read: it reproduces the 1985 HCM's two sneakers a cycle, Drew's gap model for left turns across random opposing traffic with the critical gap of 4.5 s and turning headway of 2.5 s that Fambro et al. recommended, states that the time available for permitted left turns begins only when the longest opposing lane queue has cleared, and measured a 5.1 s critical gap, 2.24 to 2.57 s turning headways and 0.88 sneakers a cycle at protected-permitted sites. It is entered as [Fambro1991]. The Signal Timing Manual's section 4.8.4 ("one or two vehicles can be served at the end of the conflicting through phase") was re-read and added to [FHWA-STM2008]. `SNEAKERS` (2) now cites both instead of an unverified HCM value.

D2. Validation cases p1 to p3. A left-only lane against 200, 400 and 600 veh/h of opposing flow at a 100 s cycle, judged against c = (3600 / C) (g_u s_p / 3600 + n_s), where g_u is the green left after the opposing queue (arrivals over the red, discharged at s) has cleared, s_p is Drew's (the Harders) gap formula and n_s the sneakers. The band runs from the measured values (5.1 s, 2.57 s, 0.88 sneakers, s 1,500) to the recommended and HCM values (4.5 s, 2.24 s, 2 sneakers, s 2,000 [FHWA-SIG2004]). The simulator gives 444, 402 and 369 left turns an hour against 351 to 483, 157 to 303 and 32 to 167: p2 and p3 fail, by up to a factor of two.

D3. Why the simulator's permissive left is too fast. Two rules let a left-turner go ahead of the opposing queue. The gap check estimates a conflicting car's arrival as distance over max(speed, 2 m/s), so a car starting from the green line 8 to 12 m out counts as 4 to 6 s away, more than the 4.1 s critical gap. And the courtesy wait (45 s) and yield fallback (90 s) count the red, so a left-turner that waited through the red takes priority over the opposing queue as the green starts; the opposing car is then held by the left-turner in the node. In a test with 500 veh/h opposing, the opposing approach carried 306 of the 500 veh/h it was offered. The left-turners do not wait inside the junction: they wait at the stop line and up to two a cycle clear on the yellow and all-red. That gives the same count per cycle as the method, so the waiting place was not changed (ASSUMPTION, stated at `sneaker`).

D4. Queue-first left turns, built and left off. `Config::queue_first_left` makes a conflicting car below its desired speed arrive by speeding up at its own acceleration, uses the [Fambro1991] critical gap of 4.5 s at signals (`TC_PERMITTED_LEFT`) and gives permissive left-turners at a signal no courtesy wait and no yield fallback. Restricted to signals, because at stop-controlled and uncontrolled nodes the same arrival estimate cut the merge of case i4 below its range. With it, p1 to p3 pass (411, 246, 120), the opposing approach of the test carries its 500 veh/h, b4 drops to 837 (range from 918) and l1 to 658. On the network it made things worse: shipped demand, PM seeds 1 to 10, removals 1.1 an hour against 0.8; the iteration-2 demand of round 32, 10.9 and 7.2 an hour on seeds 1 to 10 and 11 to 20 against 3.3 and 4.2. Undoing one part at a time (courtesy, arrival estimate, critical gap) gave 6.3, 7.4 and 6.4: each part costs capacity. Under the step rule it stays off by default; runs without it are identical to round 32. The lead's call: it is the cited behavior, and the network will likely need the protected phases the field has (left-turn arrows) before it can be switched on.

D5. l1. The expected range (851 to 1,064) applies the unsignalized major-left gap formula (tc 4.1 s, tf 2.2 s) to the hourly opposing flow over the whole cycle, while left turns can use only the green after the opposing queue has cleared. The [Fambro1991] method puts this approach at 385 to 706 per lane; the simulator gives 792 (658 with the queue-first model). So no correct behavior makes l1 pass: the simulator is already above the cited method. The range was not changed and the deviation stays unreviewed, with the comparison in its reason. What happens in the shared lane when the pocket is full: the overflowing left-turner drives to the stop line of the inner lane and turns from there beside the pocket, and through drivers behind it change lanes (MOBIL). Physically it would wait at the pocket entrance. That change would lower capacity further and was not built.

D6. Ashby Ave / Claremont Ave. OSM: northbound Claremont Ave (ways 24501620, 1503817466) is `lanes=4` with no direction split and no `turn:lanes`; southbound (way 435129985) is `lanes:backward=3`, `turn:lanes:backward=left|left;through|through;right`. The importer gives northbound two lanes and the class-rule 3-vehicle pocket, southbound three lanes with a marked left lane. The importer decides no left-turn phasing: every left is permissive unless an observed timing gives an approach a phase of its own. The 2010 PM worksheet [SafewayCollege-DEIR2011] (re-read, PDF page 482) gives northbound 53 left, 359 through, 165 right (577), southbound 272 left, 234 through, 43 right; both Claremont Ave approaches as split phases (17.0 and 17.5 s of 100 s, Ashby Ave 50.5 s), northbound coded as two lanes with no left-turn lane. So the heavy left turns there are southbound, and both Claremont approaches ran in phases of their own.

D7. The field observation, kept. The project lead relayed a resident-provided field observation (2026-11-15): northbound Claremont Ave at Ashby Ave has a left-turn arrow, so its left turn is protected. It is recorded in `signalObservations` as DATA for the phasing. Unknown and labeled ASSUMPTION pending a timing sheet: protected-only rather than protected-permissive, every period, cycle and greens by flow, the pocket length, the other approaches' phasing. With northbound in a phase of its own the southbound approach is alone in its phase, which matches the worksheet's split phasing. To represent this generically an observation may now give the phasing without timing (cycle and greens 0, `ownPhase` required; the plan keeps the structure and Webster's cycle and splits); a unit test and validation case b13 (a protected left, 450 against 452 to 602 veh/h, 2 veh/h under the lower bound, listed unreviewed) cover it. Measured: Claremont Ave northbound carries more (iteration-2 demand 437 and 456 veh/h against 379 and 411 on seeds 1 to 10 and 11 to 20; shipped seeds 1 to 10 373 against 367), Ashby Ave less, and the removals rose (iteration-2 4.6 and 4.7 an hour against 3.3 and 4.2; shipped 1.1 and 1.3 against 0.8 and 1.4). The step rule would revert it. It stays because the lead asked for the observation to be modeled; the removal rise is reported in the record.

D8. The 2010 worksheet timing at Ashby Ave / Claremont Ave was also tried on a scratch network (both Claremont approaches split, 17.0 / 17.5 s of 100 s): shipped demand removals 1.7 an hour, iteration-2 demand 4.5 (with the queue-first model on). Not kept: it is fifteen years old and only PM.

D9. Delay-feedback fit. `npm run data` rebuilt the network (6a8285d5076f9ddc) and the same demand flows (the file records the new network hash: bef51b684a8c079c). `tools/cost-loop.ts --no-stop`, resumed once from iteration 1 after a session fault, converged at iteration 3 (gap 0.090). Removals 1.1, 24.7, 13.9 and 10.6 an hour at iterations 0 to 3; the unchanged acceptance rule rejected every iteration and the demand keeps its free-flow and BPR costs. The removals of iterations 1 to 3 sit at Ashby Ave / Claremont Ave and the hillside streets behind it.

D10. Verdict. Status stays `uncalibrated`. PM: D16 fails (1.10 and 1.30 an hour on seeds 1 to 10 and 11 to 20), D5 fails (3 of 4), the through-car check fails on all four blocks, the station rule fails fitted and held out. AM: D16 passes (0.30), stations pass fitted (87%) and fail held out (74%). Midday: D16 passes. p2, p3, b13 and l1 fail with no reviewed exception. The area note says so, in visitor wording ("cars cleared from gridlock").
