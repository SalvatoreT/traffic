## Context

Round 36 traced the gridlock that keeps every delay-feedback iteration rejected to the all-way stop at Claremont Ave / Russell St and the Berkeley hills streets north of Ashby Ave, where the model has few counts. The stability rule (D16 of the baseline calibration: below 1 car an hour cleared from gridlock, mean of ten seeds) counted those clearings the same as ones on the Trapezoid's streets. Times are Pacific; the round ran on 2026-11-16.

## Decisions

D1. Reproduction. `npm run data` in the round's worktree reproduced round 36's network f9fa09a5726644d7, demand 1c798217e72fb1ba and cross-validation demand a367850a13850985; the assumptions table differs only in its generation time. PM seed 1 on them, with the new reporting, equals round 35's stored assessment run in every field but the manifest.

D2. Claremont Ave / Russell St (project lead, 2026-11-16, from a resident's field observation): the junction is an all-way stop and every approach stops, so the network there is right. It enters the area config as an `osmTagOverrides` entry that adds `stop=all` to OSM node 53082604, with the observation as its source (DATA). The importer already read the untagged junction-node stop as an all-way stop, so the network is byte for byte the same. Round 36's question whether it might be a minor-road stop is closed.

D3. Scope of the stability rule. The lead's choice, verbatim: "Judge gridlock near the Trapezoid". The limit of 1 car an hour cleared from gridlock, in D16 and in the cost loop's acceptance rule, applies to the study area, its bounding streets and the junctions on them. Gridlock clearing farther out is reported separately, with its places, as a known weakness at the model's edges and does not fail the rule. Everything else in D16 (backlog in 9 of 10 seeds, backlog growth, arterial speed) and in the cost loop's rule is unchanged, as are every other rule and every validation range.

D4. The judged zone, from data only. Core: the links and nodes the importer marks `inStudyArea` (the study area with its bounding streets, which count as inside since round 6 step G) and both ends of every such link, which puts the junctions on the bounding streets in. Zone: every node whose network distance from the core, along links in either direction of travel, is at most `judgedZone.bufferM` of the area config. A link is in the zone when it is a core link or its head node is. A car cleared from gridlock counts as near when the link it was held on (the queue head waits at the link's end) or the link it entered a node from (held inside the node) is in the zone; both cases are the end node of that link. Network distance is used because a queue spreads along links, and either direction because a held car may sit upstream or downstream of the jam's cause. An area without `judgedZone` keeps the old count (every clearing), and so does a run report without the split.

D5. Buffer 200 m (ASSUMPTION). A bounding street's queue can hold a car beyond it within the hour by filling the cross-street link that leads into its junction, so the zone has to reach the first junction beyond each bounding street. On the Rockridge network the first non-freeway link out of the core is 113 m long at the median, 200 m at the 75th percentile and 301 m at most (55 links). A queue that spills farther than that shows up as a chain of held vehicles, which D6 follows to any distance, so the buffer only needs to cover the adjacent junctions. Every report gives the verdict at half (100 m) and double (400 m) the buffer as well. The zone has 111 nodes and 329 links at 200 m.

D6. Guard against hiding a real problem: blocking chains. A far clearing counts against the rule as a near one when its blocking chain reaches the zone. The `calibrate` binary already steps the simulation in 5 s pieces; after each it reads the simulation's existing read-only diagnostics (`debug_stuck` for queue heads that wait for room on their next link, `debug_held` for vehicles held inside a node by a full exit) and keeps a graph of links joined by vehicles held at least 60 s (ASSUMPTION, long enough that a full exit, not spacing, holds them). For each car cleared in the next 5 s, the chain is the set of links connected to its link in that graph, taken in either direction. That catches far gridlock whose queue reaches into the zone and zone queues that reach out to where the car was cleared. Bounding-street travel times against the Alameda CTC ranges were not chosen as the guard: only Telegraph Ave has observed segments, the model already reads them as a calibration target of their own, and a time outside the range has many causes. They stay in the assessment. Also reported, not judged: trips waiting at origins in the zone at the end of the run, and the longest standing queue on one link of each bounding street (cars standing per lane times 6.5 m, the jam spacing the binary's node report already uses, ASSUMPTION), with the share of 5 s samples in which one of its links was at least 80% full. The freeway, which has no study-area links, is represented by its motorway links with both ends in the zone.

D7. Where it lives, and the fingerprint. The split is computed in the `calibrate` binary and written as an additive `gridlockZone` object in each seed's report. The simulator's `Metrics` and its module tree are unchanged, so native and WASM runs stay the same and the fingerprint stays f0df1aabc323 (bins are outside the hashed tree); the published proposals stay current. The engine's totals are unchanged and `near + far` equals `gridlockRemoved` in every report; a report whose split does not add up is judged on its total. The binary parses the text lines of `debug_stuck`, a hidden diagnostic; a unit test pins the format.

D8. Where D16 is judged. The ten-seed summary (`calibrate --seeds`), `tools/lib/verdict.ts` (`judgedClearings`, `computeStability`, used by `tools/assess.ts`) and `tools/cost-loop.ts` all judge the near count plus far chains into the zone, and report the total, near, far and linked far figures beside it. The cost loop now computes stability from its PM runs with the same TypeScript code as the assessment instead of reading the summary file. Its acceptance rule moved unchanged into `keptIteration` in `tools/lib/acceptance.ts` so it can be tested; the only change is that `meanRemovals` is the judged figure. The header and separator of the loop's table were one string by mistake and are now two lines.
