## Context

Round 29 tests the lead's hypothesis that the arterials around the Trapezoid are too slow on the repaired engine, so the model diverts too much through traffic onto Hillegass Ave and Colby St. The record's order applies: measure, then primitives, then signals, then route choice, then demand. All numbers are in round 29 of `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md`; this file records the decisions.

## Decisions

D1. Measure on both engines before changing anything. `calibrate --junction-recovery historical` runs the old junction policy beside the repaired one (PM, seeds 1 to 4). The observed evidence is the Alameda CTC peak-period speeds [ACTC-LOS2014; ACTC-LOS2018] on Telegraph Ave (51st St to Russell St) and College Ave (Miles Ave to Ashby Ave), the computed 2010 and 2013 intersection delays of [SafewayCollege-DEIR2011] and [TelegraphCS2013] (Synchro and HCM outputs, not measurements), and the saturation-flow ranges of [FHWA-SIG2004] that the validation suite uses. There are no observed travel times on Claremont Ave or Alcatraz Ave, no measured approach delays and no observed saturation headways at any Rockridge signal.

D2. Verdict on the hypothesis: partly right. Telegraph Ave southbound sits at or above the slowest observed year (375 to 399 s over the seed sets measured, against 300 to 376 s) and takes about 90 s more than on the historical engine, which was inside the observed range. Telegraph Ave northbound and College Ave in both directions are inside their observed ranges. The extra time sits in the SR-24 interchange (Aileen St, 56th St, 55th St) and at 52nd St and 51st St, where signals 81 to 94 m apart queue across short links that the historical engine absorbed inside its nodes. The signal capacity primitives are not at a floor because of an artifact: saturation flow (1,681 veh/h per lane) and two-lane capacity (b2, 0.95 of the range midpoint) are mid-range, and the start-up lost time is too short (a3), which makes signals faster, not slower. b9 sits at its floor because left-turners that fill the pocket block the inner lane and the simulator has no lane changes along a link; that is a missing behavior, not a parameter, and is left for a later change.

D3. Circle entry by critical headway. A circle entry was blocked by every circulating vehicle in the node from the moment it entered the circle until its tail had passed, which at 1,000 veh/h circulating left the entry blocked 86% of the time (f3 138 veh/h against 333 to 499). Each circulating vehicle now blocks the entry for t_c - t_f / 2 = 3.6 s, ending when its tail has passed, with t_f = 3600 / 1130 = 3.19 s and t_c = 5.19 s: the single-lane entry model 1130 exp(-0.001 v_c) of [Rodegerdts2007] in the Siegloch form the same reference entry gives. A circulating vehicle further away than that does not block. The `historical` policy keeps the old rule. The f cases now send the circulating vehicles on to a later exit, so they measure entry against circulating traffic as the model defines it; the note also reports the case where both streams leave by one exit lane, which adds a merge (f3 158 veh/h). f1 to f3 pass (1,089, 687, 348). Over 20 PM seeds removals are 4.7 an hour against 4.2 without it, within the seed spread; the Harmon St circle removals fall and others west of Telegraph Ave take their place. Kept as a primitive repair.

D4. Coordination tried and reverted. [FHWA-STM2008] ch. 6 quotes the MUTCD guidance that signals within 800 m along a corridor should be coordinated, and says coordinated signals share one cycle and that simultaneous offsets often work better under oversaturation. Signals joined along one street within 800 m, then within 150 m, ran on the longest member cycle with simultaneous offsets. The two-signal cases c1 and c2 rose from 0.79 and 0.75 to 1.03 and 1.00, but in the PM network (seeds 1 to 4) Telegraph Ave southbound took 445 s and 422 s against 399 s, removals rose from 4.0 to 7.25 an hour and the southbound through cars did not fall. A longer common cycle lengthens the queues on the 81 to 94 m links. Reverted under the stop rule.

D5. College Ave / Claremont Ave / 62nd St: observed timing tried and reverted. The 2010 PM worksheet [SafewayCollege-DEIR2011] gives a 110 s cycle with 62nd St eastbound 15 s, College Ave 41 s and Claremont Ave 19 s and 19 s as split phases. The adaptive plan gives 62nd St about 18 to 25 s. With the observed plan, 62nd St eastbound fell from 198 to about 150 veh/h (59 counted) and Hillegass Ave southbound through cars by about 10, but Forest St eastbound rose by about 20 and removals rose: 3.6 to 5.7 an hour over PM seeds 1 to 10 without the circle repair, 4.7 to 6.5 over seeds 1 to 20 with it. The rule chosen is therefore: an adaptive signal keeps its Webster plan unless an observed timing exists, and an observed timing is applied only when it does not make stability worse; for this signal it does, so the observation is not added to the area config. A bound on the minor-street split with no source was not tried, because it would be a tuning knob.

D6. Route choice unchanged. Drivers choose a route at departure on the current costs and 46% re-plan en route [Guin2021] with an indifference band [Srinivasan2000]. The guided share (0 or 1) and the switch band (0.4) move the southbound through cars by less than the seed spread; only the local-street cost factor, an ASSUMPTION, moves them much, and 1.5 still leaves Hillegass Ave southbound at about 200 while removals and Telegraph Ave times rise. The diversion is a response to the simulated costs at departure, so no route-choice parameter was changed.

D7. Demand unchanged. The demand and the network are those of round 28; `npm run data` reproduces them. The status stays `uncalibrated`.

## Risks

- Telegraph Ave southbound stays slower than observed, so the model still sends too many through cars to the side streets. The likely repairs (lane changes along a link, offsets set per direction at the SR-24 interchange) are changes of model behavior that need their own sources.
- PM removals vary by about 2 an hour between ten-seed sets on the same state; differences of that size are noise.
