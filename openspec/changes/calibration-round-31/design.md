## Context

Round 30 found that most of the excess southbound through cars on Hillegass Ave and Colby St, and the eastbound excess on 62nd St and Forest St, drive a route the router itself ranked costlier at departure. The cause was the form of the route-choice error: the walk scaled each candidate's whole remaining cost by a fresh factor in 0.92 to 1.08 (`ROUTE_NOISE`, unsourced) at every node, so a route's chance depended on the number of decision points along the way. This round replaces that rule with a sourced dispersion model, then refits the demand and evaluates. The order of `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md` applies. All numbers are in round 31 of the record; this file records the decisions.

## Decisions

D1. Model: probit route choice with perceived costs drawn once per driver. Sheffi (1985, ch. 11.2, read in the author's posted PDF) gives the perceived time of link a as normal with mean t_a and variance β t_a, non-overlapping segments perceived independently, so a route's perceived time is normal with variance β times its time and routes that share links are correlated (eqs. 11.12 to 11.14c); Daganzo and Sheffi (1977) proposed the model (Crossref record and abstract read; the paper itself was not opened). The simulator applies it per driver: every arc (a trip's first link, or a movement together with the link it enters) gets a standard normal deviate fixed for the trip, and the driver's perceived cost of the arc is its current cost plus sqrt(β x cost) times that deviate, truncated at zero as the book allows. The deviate comes from a hash of the driver's seed and the arc, mapped to the midpoint quantile of one of 4,096 equal-probability bins (quantiles by Acklam's rational approximation in f64 with libm), so a driver perceives the same arc the same way in a paired scenario and in native and WASM runs. Burrell (1968) is the uniform-distribution variant named in the book; it was not read and is not cited separately.

D2. Exact shortest path per driver. The driver routes by a forward Dijkstra over links on its perceived costs, stopping at the first goal link settled (`route::Perceived`). The per-node rule cannot be kept as an approximation because its bias is the defect. A* with a lower-bound heuristic was considered and not built: with β of a few seconds, the lower bound of a 10 to 25 s arc at the deepest quantile is zero, so it would not prune. A trip waiting at its entry kept recomputing its route at every step; its route depends only on the costs in force, so it is kept until they change (`spawn_cache`, cleared wherever the router's tables are). That cut the searches of a 25-minute PM run from 81,000 to 33,000. A PM run takes about 39 s instead of 30 s.

D3. One perception for every routing decision of a trip. Departure routes, the guided drivers' re-planning and its comparison of the current and the new route (`path_cost` sums the same perceived arcs), re-planning around a blocked link, re-planning after an intervention, the legs of an assumed movement (to its entry link, then from its exit link), the legs of a cut turn's bypass, and the routes of the initial loading all use the driver's perceived costs. Without perception (the `NO_NOISE` seed of cyclists and of the fallbacks, or a dispersion of 0) the table walk is kept and gives the plain shortest path as before.

D4. What stays on mean costs. The choice of which assumed movement a flow makes (`pick_via`, free-flow costs against the detour tolerance) and the ranking of a cut turn's alternatives and bypasses (`pick_cut_via`, the router's current costs) remain driver-independent: they say which counted movement or bypass a flow keeps, a property of the plan, and the perceived costs have the mean costs as their mean. A route through an assumed movement that loops where the plain route does not is still rejected; at departure and in the initial loading the driver then tries the movement on mean costs before dropping it, as re-planning already did. Without this, 1 of 60 trips in the corridor test lost a movement that every trip made before, because a U-turn back to the arterial was perceived as cheaper.

D5. The static assignment loads by the same model. `tools/lib/assign.ts` previously mimicked the per-node walk. It now follows the book's loading algorithm: each draw samples a perceived cost for every link and every movement (normal, variance `dispersion` x cost, truncated at zero) and loads each flow on its shortest path on that draw; the drivers of one draw share it. The link and the movement are perceived separately there because the static costs carry no measured movement term that can be negative; the variance per route is the same. The config key `noise` is replaced by `dispersion` in both the free and the successive-averages assignment.

D6. Validation cases. r1 and r2: two routes of equal cost; route A has 1 or 20 junctions, each with a one-way side link onto route B costing 2.5 standard deviations of the probit difference more. The trip is long enough (about 2,000 x β s before the side links) that the old factor still reaches past that extra cost at each junction. The perceived-cost model must keep 0.45 to 0.55 of the drivers on A (band ASSUMPTION). r3 to r6: two routes with no link in common, B costlier by 15, 30, 60 and 120 s on a 600 s trip; the share on B must be within 0.03 of Phi(-d / sqrt(β (cA + cB))) (eq. 11.20, band ASSUMPTION for 4,000 drivers). Each note gives the per-node rule's share. At β = 2: r1 0.496 (old 0.418), r2 0.499 (old 0.020), r3 to r6 0.382, 0.270, 0.108, 0.012 against 0.381, 0.273, 0.116, 0.010 (old 0.369, 0.244, 0.081, 0.000).

D7. Test bands restated from the cost model. Three tests asserted shares set under the old rule. Closing one of two parallel cut-throughs: B is 18 s cheaper than the arterial on measured costs on a 135 s trip, so the probit share of the arterial is about 0.23; the band is now at least 60% on B and at most 40% to the arterial (measured 75%). The same flow with an assumed movement into the closed street: same band (measured 79%). A cut counted turn's dogleg: from the end of Mid St's first block the dogleg takes about 50 s and the way round by East Blvd about 70 s, a probit share of 0.10 for the longer way; at least 75% must take the dogleg and every trip must stay on Mid St to First St and leave by Top Ave (measured 73 of 85). A through trip on a 70 s arterial with a local row 45 s costlier: at most 2% on the local row (Phi gives 0.008), over an hour instead of 10 minutes. The car-only golden metrics of the corridor were regenerated. Trace on and off stay identical (the trace tests and the parity check pass).

D8. The dispersion value. No source gives β for urban street networks (the book's examples set β = 1 time unit for illustration), so `ROUTE_DISPERSION_S` is an ASSUMPTION chosen by a sweep. Each value was run with its own demand build (the static assignment uses the same β), on PM and AM seeds 1 to 4, and judged only on fitted evidence: removals per hour (D16, below 1), the headline shares (D5), the fitted stations' GEH share and the six arterial travel times against the ACTC ranges. Held-out stations and the four through-car ranges were not used; the through cars are reported beside the sweep. The values are in round 31 of the record. Chosen: β = 2 s. The values 3 and 4 fail D16 in the PM peak (25 and 1.75 removals an hour); of the stable values (0.5, 1, 2), 2 has the lowest PM headline error (8.0 points against 9.9 and 13.0); 1 is the runner-up. The through-car check, not used, favors 1.

D9. Demand and fit. `npm run data` rebuilt both demand views on the new assignment; the network (69f62ad981d0b8fa) and the frozen split are unchanged. The delay-feedback loop converged at iteration 3 (gap 0.077). Every iteration had 4 to 7 times the removals of the free-flow costs (6.2, 5.4, 8.0 an hour against 1.2), so its rule restored the demand (edaae9760f406e08). Iteration 2 had the southbound through cars inside their ranges (110 and 115), which repeats round 30's finding that the counts are reached only by loading the arterials past stability.

D10. Not attempted: the College Ave southbound delay at College Ave / Claremont Ave / 62nd St. The task allowed it only if time remained and the evidence pointed there; the round stops at the honest state with the southbound through cars still above their ranges.

## Risks

- The dispersion is an assumption fitted to this area's counts; it may not transfer.
- Perception per driver multiplies routing work; a larger network or a much larger guided share may need a goal-directed search.
- Drivers now take a costlier route with a probability set by its cost, not by the number of junctions; a short trip has a larger relative error than a long one, which is what the cited model says but which no local data confirms.
