# Proposal search

`cargo run --release -p traffic-sim --bin explore` searches for packages of interventions that cut cut-through traffic on the study area's residential and service streets without piling more traffic onto any one block. It writes `public/data/<area>.proposals.json` (read by the app's Proposals tab) and a short report, `data/proposals/<area>.md`. The design is in `openspec/changes/proposals/design.md`; the code is in `crates/sim/src/explore/`.

```
cargo run --release -p traffic-sim --bin explore -- --area rockridge \
  --periods pm_peak,am_peak --beam 3 --depth 8 --candidates 40 \
  [--seeds 1-3] [--confirm-seeds 101-110 | --no-confirm] [--screen-minutes 30] [--minutes 60] [--budget-evals N] [--jobs N] \
  [--harm-weight 0.1] [--expand 16] [--finalists 12] [--options 6] [--objectives all|LIST] [--out FILE] [--report FILE]
```

The default run is the whole job. It searches and rechecks on seeds 1 to 3, then reruns the chosen shortlist on ten seeds that took no part in the search (`--confirm-seeds 101-110`), and those ten paired runs are what the file publishes. It replaces `public/data/<area>.proposals.json` and the report: the file holds one list.

To add the pictures the cards draw to a file that was written without them, without searching again:

```
cargo run --release -p traffic-sim --bin explore -- --area rockridge --annotate --jobs 4
```

It reads `public/data/<area>.proposals.json` (or `--out FILE`) and checks every group before it writes anything. A group must record the network and demand hashes, the simulator fingerprint, the reporting-method version, its run settings and the run length, and each must match the build in use. A group that differs, or that lacks any of them (a version 4 group), stops the command with a message naming the group, and the file stays as it was: such figures need a full search, because pictures from one simulator must not sit beside numbers from another. For a compatible group it re-simulates only the baseline and the published packages for the picture period, with the group's seeds and run length, and writes `picture` and `pictureAll` into every package. The numbers and their provenance are not touched. It does not write the report.

The simulator results change whenever the network, the demand or the model changes, so rerun the search after any of them. The output is not meant to be edited by hand.

## Limits on the candidates (optional)

The proposals are one list, searched without limits: every tool except new traffic lights, anywhere in the area. The 2025 neighborhood pilot's limits are not a criterion, and no file in the repository defines a limit profile. The code still accepts one for experiments: `--profile ID` with `--profiles FILE`, a list of `{ id, title, note, excludeTypes, excludeBoundary }`. It removes the excluded intervention types from the candidates and, with `excludeBoundary`, every candidate on a bounding street or at an intersection with one. A run with `--profile` writes its own group next to the others in the file; nothing published depends on it. Files written before 2026-11-15 hold two groups, one of them under those limits. The app shows their options as one list and every package once.

## What it measures

Two kinds of car are counted, and every figure says which. **Through cars** are cars that cross the area without a trip that starts or ends in it. **All cars** are every car on a block, through cars and the local trips of people who start or end a trip in the area. Through cars are part of all cars. They are never added to them, and a through number is never compared with an all-cars number.

- **Goal.** Through cars an hour across all the area's residential and service streets together (`metrics().cutThrough.vehiclesPerHour`), each car counted once, averaged over the periods.
- **Side effect shown on the proposal card (like for like).** The side-street block with the largest raw increase in through cars an hour, both directions summed, averaged over the same periods and seeds as the goal, with its before and after values. This is the raw maximum: ties go to the lowest block position. It is separate from the block behind the search penalty (below), which can be another block. A block with a wide noise band can have the smaller raw increase. The card shows the raw maximum even when its interval includes zero, with that status, and never turns an unresolved increase into "no gain".
- **Search penalty (all traffic).** The ranking penalizes the largest increase in all cars (through and local trips together, both directions summed) on any one block, taken over the periods, after a noise band, `max(2 x the standard deviation of the block's baseline flow across seeds, 10 cars an hour)` (both constants in `explore/mod.rs`), floored at zero. The band is an assumed dead band for screening, not an uncertainty interval. The file reports this block and value as `adjustedPenalty`, beside the raw maximum. The search deliberately uses all traffic here: residents experience every car on their street, so a filter that sends local trips down the next street counts against it. The cards report through cars so that the two headline figures are the same kind of car; the all-traffic change on each period's worst block, with its split into through cars and local trips, is in the card's details.
- **Context** (information only): all-car flow and speed change on the arterials that run through the study area, mean time of trips that start or end inside it, and pedestrian and bicycle conflict counts.

Flow figures, through and all, use the same window: after a warm-up of the first 25% of the run, at most 10 minutes. Through cars per block come from the simulator's per-link cut-through counters (the ones behind the map's cut-through overlay) over that window. The area-wide through count covers the whole run from an empty network and is divided by the run length, the same as the metric in the app, so the area figure and the per-block figures differ slightly in window and are not meant to be added up.

## Candidates

Select-link analysis of the baseline ranks the in-study blocks and intersections by cut-through trips. The top `--candidates` locations get every intervention the app offers there and that fits: modal filter on a block; modal filter at an intersection and diagonal diverters (both orientations, four-leg intersections), only where every street is residential or service; one-way in each direction on two-way blocks; speed hump at mid-block where none exists; traffic circle at four-leg uncontrolled or stop-controlled intersections of local streets; 15 and 20 mph limits where the limit is higher; all-way stop where the control is a two-way stop or none; 60 and 120 s cycles at existing signals that cut-through passes. Turn bans cover the heaviest cut-through movements from an arterial onto a local street. A proposal never adds a signal. Equivalent candidates are merged.

### Turn-ban packages

Turn-ban candidates cover single movements, so two packages were entered by hand for the search of 2026-11-18 (user decision, 2026-11-18) and measured on seeds 101 to 110 over three periods, on the network and demand of that search (baseline 508.4 through cars an hour):

- Banning the four turns from Claremont Avenue and Alcatraz Avenue onto Hillegass Avenue removes 29.0 through cars an hour (95% interval 17 to 41; 5.7%). Most of them move to Colby Street: one block (McAuley Street to 60th Street) gains 133 cars an hour of all traffic in the PM peak.
- Banning the same turns onto both Hillegass Avenue and Colby Street (eight bans) removes 63.9 (55 to 73; 12.6%) and adds 100 cars an hour in the PM peak to 60th Street between Claremont Avenue and Colby Street. Through traffic also moves to Dana Street.

Neither is published as an option: two filters on 63rd Street remove 160.8 with a smaller rise on any one block. The eight-ban package has more changes than the search depth of 6, so the search dropped it and it was measured with `explore --check`. The figures are in `openspec/changes/archive/2026-11-19-calibration-round-44/evidence/search-c5/tb-check.md`. Generating whole-street and paired-street ban sets as search candidates is not built.

The published set since 2026-11-20 is the search on the round 52 demand (baseline 522.5 through cars an hour, evidence in `openspec/changes/archive/2026-11-20-calibration-round-52/evidence/search/`). These two packages were not measured again on that demand, so their figures above belong to the earlier one.

## Search

Every candidate is screened alone on the first period and seed at `--screen-minutes`. A beam of the best `--beam` packages is then extended one intervention at a time, up to `--depth`, with the best `--expand` singles plus repair moves (candidates on or next to the blocks that gained traffic). The score is the reduction in through cars in percent minus `--harm-weight` percent per car an hour of the all-traffic penalty above; the weight is an assumption. Inside the beam, partial packages are ranked with a harm weight that grows linearly from zero for one intervention to the full weight at `--depth` (an assumption). Later interventions repair what earlier ones displace, so a partial package's harm overstates the harm of the package it grows into; a line stops when no package of the next level beats the previous level's best at the new level's weight. The published score, the finalists and the options always use the full weight. Screening shares seeds with the baseline. The search keeps every evaluated package and takes the non-dominated ones on (reduction, harm, number of interventions); up to `--finalists` of them are rerun for `--minutes` over all periods and seeds. Only those results are published.

A package is rejected before simulation if an intervention is invalid or any in-study node loses its way in from a gate or out to one (nodes already cut off in the baseline do not count). It is rejected after a run if unroutable trips exceed the baseline's. Gridlock removals and entry backlog beyond the baseline's by more than 20% plus 10 vehicles, and 5% of spawned trips, are also rejected, because removed or queued vehicles would otherwise read as a reduction. Results do not depend on `--jobs`.

## Known packages (seeds)

`data/proposals/<area>.seeds.json` lists scenario links of packages worth rechecking: `{ link, source, note, added, lastPublished, group, size, changePct, score }`, `source` being `search` (published by a run) or `hand`. Every run reads it, drops the entries its profile forbids, that fail the access check or that no longer decode on the current network (each is reported, with its reason, in the report and in `computed.knownPackages`), and rechecks all the others in full over every period and seed, outside the screening budget. They compete for the published options on equal terms. Each seed and each package one intervention smaller than it is also screened, and the best of them, taken in turn from each seed's family, start a beam of their own. At the end the run adds the options it published to the file. A package already there keeps its first date, source and note and takes the new figures. Over the cap (`cap` in the file, 20 by default, because each seed costs one full recheck) the oldest search-published entries that the run did not publish go first, the weakest first among equals. Entries added by hand are never dropped.

```
explore --area rockridge --add-seed '#v1;a=rockridge;p=pm_peak;s=...' --note "why it matters" [--published-on 2026-11-14]
explore --area rockridge --seed-proposals public/data/rockridge.proposals.json   # every search option in a proposals file
explore --area rockridge --seed-labels labels.json                               # [{ "labels": [...], "note", "date", "group" }] from an old report
explore --area rockridge --check '#v1;...' [--check-subsets] [--check-singles]
```

`--check` scores a package with the search's own code: whether the profile allows each intervention, whether the candidate list holds it, the access check, the full recheck over every period and seed, and with `--check-subsets` the screening score of every subset (which shows whether a beam could reach the package), with `--check-singles` the rank of each of its interventions among all the single interventions. `--known FILE` reads and writes another seeds file (use it for experiments), `--no-known` ignores the file.

## Choosing the options

From the rechecked finalists the tool keeps those that no other finalist beats on reduction, harm and size, and drops any whose reduction is within noise of a simpler one. When a package that no earlier search published is within noise on through cars of one that was (a known package with a `lastPublished` date), with no more harm and no more interventions, the earlier package is kept and the new one dropped, so the list does not churn between runs. A published package falls to dominance only when a new one beats it by more than the noise. Two options differ when the interval of their per-seed difference in through cars (see below) lies on one side of zero and the mean gap is at least the 1% floor of `GOAL_NOISE_FLOOR_PCT` (an assumed practical floor); with fewer paired runs than the method needs, only that floor applies and the file says the evidence is insufficient.

Near-duplicates are then folded into one (`explore/similar.rs`). Two packages are similar when any of these holds:

1. They act at the same places and differ only in the variant at a place (which leg a filter sits on, a filter or a diverter at the same junction).
2. One differs from the other by at most one change (one added, dropped or swapped) and neither beats the other on through cars under the paired comparison (`beats`: the paired interval lies above zero and the gap is at least the 1% floor; with too few pairs, the 1% floor alone).
3. Their through-car changes on the side-street blocks are nearly the same: `sum |a - b| / (sum |a| + sum |b|)` over the blocks, mean over periods and seeds, is at most 0.10 (`PICTURE_DISTANCE_MAX`, an assumption; it is 0 for equal changes, 1 for opposite ones, and about 0.1 when one is the other scaled by 1.22).

Of a similar group one package is kept: the one with the larger reduction if it wins the paired comparison, otherwise the one with fewer changes, then the smaller largest all-car rise, then the one an earlier search published, then the earlier finalist. The rest become **variants** of the kept option: tweaks the card lists in plain lines (`Use X instead of Y`, `Add: X`, `Drop: X`), with their through-car figures from the runs that chose them and a scenario link. Variants are not drawn in the picture, have no share path and no stored result. `computed.similarity` records every fold and its reason, and the report has a "Similar packages" section.

Options are chosen for named objectives (`explore/objectives.rs`), and each published option lists the objectives it is the best for in `objectives` (ids `most-removed`, `no-closures`, `least-detour`, `fewest-changes`). Each objective has a rule, and every threshold in them is an assumption:

1. Most cut-through removed: the search score, through cars removed less the light penalty for the largest all-car rise on one block.
2. No street closures: the same score among packages made only of measures that close nothing to cars (Slow Streets, one-ways, all-way stops, traffic circles, speed humps, turn bans). Its beam may combine only those candidates.
3. Least detour for residents: among packages that remove at least half (`DETOUR_REDUCTION_FLOOR`) of the most removed in its pool, the one with the smallest added trip time per trip for trips that start or end inside the study area. A difference the paired comparison cannot resolve (the interval of the per-seed difference includes zero, or with fewer than ten shared seeds a gap of at most `DETOUR_TIE_S`, 1 s) goes to the package that removes more through cars, then to the one with fewer changes. The beam for this goal ranks partial packages by detour once they pass the floor (half of the best reduction screened so far) and by reduction below it. The detour is the mean trip time of completed car trips with an end inside the study area, scenario minus baseline on the same seed, averaged over the periods (`residentsDetour.secondsPerTrip`, with `effect` and `vehicleMinutesPerHour`). Only trips that complete in the run are timed.
4. Fewest changes: the same score among packages of at most two changes.

Each objective grows its own beam over the shared screening cache, and its finalists join the pool that is rechecked. Each objective then picks its winner from the packages it may win, after the filters above are applied inside that pool, so a better package that closes a street cannot hide the best one that does not. A package that wins several objectives is listed once with all its tags. The rest of the list, up to `--options` (default 6), is the best remaining by through cars removed that is not similar to an option already chosen and that no other published option displaces. An option that wins no goal is dropped when another published option removes at least as many through cars, or cannot be told from it on the paired comparison (`beats`), and its largest all-car rise on one block is smaller by more than the noise of the two rises: the half widths of their paired intervals combined, or `NOISE_FLOOR_VEH_H` (10 cars an hour, an assumption) when either has none. Goal winners are never dropped. With confirmation seeds the rule runs again on the confirmed figures, which are the published ones: the worst displaced option goes, the rest are judged again, and the list is not refilled with finalists that were not confirmed. `computed.displacement` and the report list every drop with its reason, `basis` (`search` or `confirmation`) and the `seeds` its figures come from. `explore --reapply-displacement FILE [--report FILE]` applies the same pass to a finished proposals file and its report without a search: it drops the options, renumbers them `p1`..`pN`, rebuilds `optionComparisons`, labels earlier drop records as search-seed figures and leaves the seeds file alone. Options are ordered by through cars removed an hour, largest first. Ties go to fewer changes, then the smaller largest all-car rise. With confirmation seeds the order follows the confirmed figures. The app sorts older files the same way when it reads them. A file written before objectives has no `objectives` or `residentsDetour`; the app then shows no tags and no detour line.

## Reading the file

`public/data/<area>.proposals.json` follows the contract in the design. Interpretations worth knowing:

- `cutThrough.changePct` is negative for a reduction. `baselineVehPerHour` and `scenarioVehPerHour` are through cars an hour across the area's side streets, averaged over the periods and seeds, and `effect` and `changePctEffect` give the paired change with its interval (see Uncertainty). The file is version 5; groups written by version 2 (vehicle-miles) or version 3 (one all-traffic worst block beside through cars) are shown by the app as out of date until they are computed again. A version 4 file is still read: its figures are shown and marked as lacking provenance and intervals, and its one worst block is described as the block the search flagged with its noise band.
- `harm.through.largestIncrease` is the block with the largest raw average increase in through cars an hour, averaged over the periods and seeds (`block`, `baselineVehPerHour`, `scenarioVehPerHour`, `increaseVehPerHour`, and `effect`, the paired change on that block). `harm.through.adjustedPenalty` is the block behind the search penalty and the penalty after its band (`block` is null when no block passes its band). `blocksAboveBand` counts blocks past their assumed band and `blocksResolvedIncrease` counts blocks whose seed interval is above zero.
- `byPeriod[p].cutThrough` is the same triple as the overall `cutThrough` for one period. `byPeriod[p].allTraffic` is about all cars, not through cars: `largestIncrease` is the block with the largest raw all-traffic increase in that period (`block`, `baselineVehPerHour`, `scenarioVehPerHour`, `increaseVehPerHour`, `throughIncreaseVehPerHour` for how much of the change is through cars, and `effect`), `adjustedPenalty` the block behind the penalty in that period, and `blocksAboveBand` how many blocks pass their assumed band. The local trips' share of the change is the all-car change minus `throughIncreaseVehPerHour`.
- `context.boundingStreets` lists the named primary, secondary and tertiary streets with in-study links, summed over their in-study links. `flowChangePct` compares summed flow, `speedChangeMph` the flow-weighted mean speed.
- `picture` holds, for one period, the through cars an hour on each link that has any, today (`base`) and with the package (`scen`), parallel to `links` (link ids), integers, averaged over the group's seeds with the same counters and window as the block figures. The period is the PM peak when it was searched, else the first period. The card draws the change on each block (both directions summed) with the interventions on top. It is absent from files written before pictures; the card then shows none. `computed.minutes` is the run length the group used.
- `pictureAll` has the same shape and period as `picture` but counts all cars (through cars and local trips) on the links of the side-street blocks the search measures, today and with the package, averaged over the same seeds. The card's street panel and "Streets that change" table show it beside the through cars. It is absent from files written before it was added; the card then says all cars are not in the file.
- `link` opens the package on the first searched period.
- `variants` (optional, on a search option) lists the packages folded into it: `link`, `differs` (plain lines), `why` (`same-places`, `one-change` or `same-picture`), `origin` and `cutThrough` (before, after and change in through cars an hour, with the `seeds` and `role` of the runs that chose it, which are not the option's confirmation runs).
- `computed.similarity` holds the rule, the picture-distance limit and every fold: the package's link, the link it folded into, the reason, and whether the option it folded into was published.
- `optionComparisons` compares each option with the next one in the list: `option` against `against`, the extra points of through cars the first cuts.
- `computed.networkHash` and `demandHash` are the first 16 hex characters of the SHA-256 of the file bytes of `public/data/<area>.network.json` and `<area>.demand.json`. `calibration` is the status in `public/data/areas.json`.

Scenario links come from a Rust port of `src/lib/hash.ts`; `crates/sim/tests/fixtures/links.json` holds strings made by the TypeScript encoder (`npx tsx tools/gen-proposal-link-fixtures.ts`) that the Rust tests must match.

## Reference packages

`data/proposals/<area>.references.json` lists packages written by hand, evaluated by the same full recheck and published after the search options with `kind: "reference"` and a `note`. They skip the non-dominated filter and the option cap. Places are given by street name so the file survives network rebuilds: `{ "type": "modal_filter", "at": ["A", "B"] }` for a node, `{ "type": "modal_filter", "on": "A", "between": ["B", "C"] }` for a block, and for diverters `"connect": [["A", "south"], ["B", "west"]]` (a pair of legs that stay connected). The resolver fails with a clear message when the names match no node or more than one. Search entries have `kind: "search"`.

## Uncertainty and provenance

Every final comparison pairs each scenario run with the baseline run of the same period and seed. An effect is the mean of the per-seed differences with a two-sided 95% Student t interval on them [NIST-Handbook, 7.2.2.1 and 1.3.5.3; critical values 1.3.6.7.2]. For the headline the periods are averaged with equal weights within each seed first, so the pairing is by seed and the correlation between periods is inside each difference. Percent changes divide by the baseline mean. An option-to-option difference pairs the two scenarios on the same seeds. The interval covers seed variability with the network, demand and model fixed. It does not cover calibration error, demand error or day-to-day variation in real traffic. The interval for the raw-maximum block is for a block picked after looking at all of them, so it is not adjusted for that choice.

- **Seeds.** `--seeds` takes a list or a range (`1-10`): a continuous range with a fixed increment, the same on the baseline and every package [TfL-TMG4, 7.5.1; WebTAG-M3.1, C.9.1 and C.10.1]. The comparison makes a statement only with at least 10 paired runs (`MIN_PAIRS`; ASSUMPTION: 10 is the starting point [WebTAG-M3.1, C.9.1] gives, not a proof of precision). With fewer, effects are reported with `status` `insufficient` and the search screens by its practical floors alone. There is no adaptive stopping rule: the schedule is fixed, and the interval's width is published so a reader can judge whether 10 runs were enough [WebTAG-M3.1, C.3.14].
- **Status.** An effect's `status` is `increase` or `decrease` when the interval is on one side of zero, `unresolved` when it includes zero and `insufficient` with too few runs. An option comparison uses `better`, `worse`, `unresolved` and `insufficient`.
- **Practical floors are separate.** The block noise band (above), its 10 cars an hour floor and the 1% reduction floor are assumptions for screening. They never appear as intervals.
- **Exploratory or confirmed.** The finalist rechecks choose the options, so their intervals are exploratory (`computed.estimation.selection.estimates` and each proposal's `evidence.role` say `selection`). `--confirm-seeds 101-110` freezes the chosen shortlist and reruns it, and the references, on seeds that took no part in screening, rechecking or choosing (the ranges must not overlap); the file then records the shortlist, both seed lists and role `confirmation`. A package changed after seeing confirmation results needs a new confirmation range.
- **Provenance.** Each group's `computed` records the network and demand hashes, `simulator` (the fingerprint of the simulator build), `reportMethod` (`paired-t-2`, bumped when the pairing, interval or selection rules change, because the fingerprint does not cover this module), `settings`, `sourceRevision` (the git revision, `-dirty` when the tree had changes), the periods, the seeds of the published estimates and the run length. The app, the annotator, the text pages, the preview cards and the pictures treat a mismatch as stale and a missing field as unverified.
- **Cost.** The final recheck runs every finalist and known package on every period and seed, so ten seeds cost about 3.3 times what three did. Screening and the beam are unchanged. Lower `--finalists` or the seeds file cap to stay inside a time limit.
