## Sources checked and not usable

- Oakland Socrata catalogue (`data.oaklandca.gov`, queries: traffic count, traffic volume, ADT, speed survey, counts, crash): no ADT, segment volume or speed-survey dataset exists; the only counts are the annual multi-modal turning-movement counts above (36 intersections, mostly outside the area).
- Berkeley open data (`data.cityofberkeley.info`): catalogue search for traffic/count/volume returns only signals and conduits; no traffic counts found.
- Alameda CTC, MTC, Replica public summaries, Oakland speed surveys: not searched in depth / not found; no data used.
- Caltrans Traffic_AADT ArcGIS layer and GIS service list: no ramp-volume layer exists in the services listed (CHhighway folder); ramp ADT is only published as the District workbooks above, so those are used.
- Caltrans AADT on SR-13 Claremont Ave (PM 10.79, AADT 32,500 back / 23,500 ahead, 2023-24 count year) is the nearest state count to the Claremont gate but sits at Ashby/Tunnel Rd, so it is scaled (below).

## Method

1. Gate peak volume. DATA: gates on Telegraph (N and S legs) and Alcatraz (W leg) take PM peak-hour entering/leaving volumes from the Telegraph & Alcatraz counts (mean of 4 counts); the leg is chosen from the gate's compass direction relative to the count intersection and street name. If several gate nodes share a leg the volume is split among them by whether they can emit/absorb traffic. ASSUMPTION: the south Telegraph gate reuses the S-leg volume as proxy.
2. DATA/ASSUMPTION: Claremont gate: Caltrans SR-13 AADT ([CaltransAADT]) 23,500 x 0.6 (assumed attenuation) = 14,100 ADT.
3. ASSUMPTION: all other surface gates use per-class default ADT (primary 14,000; secondary 9,000; tertiary 5,000; residential 800; service 150). Freeway gates are handled in step 9.
4. ASSUMPTION: peak hour = ADT x K (K = 0.09, urban arterial range 0.08-0.10; K and D definitions in [FHWA-TMG2022], see also [HCM7]; the 0.08-0.10 range itself is not source-verified in `docs/references.md`); directional inbound share 0.45 (PM). Count-based gates use the measured split.
5. ASSUMPTION: AM and midday gate volumes = PM peak-hour volume x 0.85 and 0.62; inbound shares 0.58 (AM), 0.50 (midday), 0.45 (PM). Uncalibrated.
6. DATA + ASSUMPTION: local trips (LODES per [LODES]; trip-rate conventions per [ITE-TGM11] are not used; the rates below are assumptions). LODES resident workers and jobs per block map to the nearest in-area node on a residential link. ASSUMPTION rates: 3.2 daily trips per resident worker, 3.5 per job, vehicle mode share 0.62, peak-hour share 0.085/0.06/0.095 (AM/midday/PM), origin share of resident trips 0.7/0.5/0.3, 80% of local trips external. DATA: each block's external trips are split by LODES home/work direction sector, then to gates by angular proximity (Gaussian, sigma 45 deg) x gate in/out target. Remaining 20% are internal, gravity weighted by attraction x exp(-d/1 km) (ASSUMPTION). Local use of any gate is capped at 50% of its target.
7. ASSUMPTION: through trips (gate to gate). Residual gate inflow/outflow after local trips is balanced by Furness/IPF ([DemingStephan1940]; [Furness1965] is UNVERIFIED; successive-approximation relative [Fratar1954]) (outflow targets scaled to total inflow). Seed = target outflow x turn factor: straight-through (>=135 deg) 1.0, adjacent (75-135 deg) 0.6, oblique 0.3, U-turn-like (<40 deg) 0.05; x3 for same-street pairs. This gravity/turn-plausibility form is a modeling assumption and is not fitted.
8. Freeway ramps. Topology (generic, no area-specific code): freeway-class links (`config.freewayClasses`, here `motorway`, including the former `motorway_link` ramps) form the freeway; a ramp is a surface node touched by both freeway and surface links. Off-ramp = a freeway link arrives (freeway gate to surface); on-ramp = a freeway link leaves. The feeding gate of an off-ramp is the pure-source freeway gate reached walking upstream; the receiving gate of an on-ramp is the pure-sink gate reached downstream. Rockridge extract (4 ramps; SR-24 here runs east-west): freeway gate 243 (east end, WB traffic) -> WB off-ramp -> Telegraph Ave/Aileen St (node 247); freeway gate 422 (west end, EB traffic) -> EB off-ramp -> Claremont Ave/Clifton St (node 251); Claremont Ave/Hudson St (node 226) -> WB on-ramp -> gate 418 (west end); Telegraph Ave/56th St (node 52) -> EB on-ramp -> gate 417 (east end). Gate 244 is a short stub downstream of 243 and carries no demand. The 51st/52nd-Grove ramps (PM R2.09) lie west of the extract, so those streets stay ordinary surface gates (their Caltrans ramp ADT, 2019: 15,200 off / 18,300 on, is not used).
9. DATA + ASSUMPTION: ramp volumes. DATA: ramp ADT from [CaltransRampADT], matched to each network ramp by street name (`streetMatch`) and type (OFF TO / ON FR); latest count year, which is 2012 for all four ramps (ASSUMPTION: no growth since). Peak hour = ADT x 0.09 (ASSUMPTION, as in step 4) x period volume factor (step 5), so the PM peak totals 3,100 veh/h one-way over the four ramps; a ramp with no published count would use `ramps.defaultAdt` = 3,000 (ASSUMPTION, not triggered). Mainline SR-24 AADT at the same postmile (about 134,000-137,000, [CaltransAADT]) is context only: freeway-mainline-to-mainline trips never touch surface streets and are not generated.
10. ASSUMPTION: ramp destination/origin choice. For each ramp, 15% of trips (`ramps.localShare`) have an in-area LODES node at the surface end, 85% a surface gate. Within each group, weights = attraction x direction plausibility: attraction = gate in/outflow target (gates) or LODES trip attraction (local nodes); plausibility = exp(-(dTheta/90 deg)^2), floor 0.03, where dTheta is the angle between the bearing ramp->destination and the bearing ramp->study-area center, so freeway-origin trips mostly head into the neighborhood and beyond (north, east, west) and few go back south. No distance term. A gate takes at most 40% of its target as ramp trips (`ramps.maxShareOfGate`, water-filling). Ramp trips count toward the surface gate flows: they are subtracted from the residual that the Furness step (step 7) balances; ramp trips with a local end replace part of that block's external local trips. Which streets the trips use is left to the simulator. The qualitative targets in `data/calibration/` (ramp traffic continues north, little ends on College Ave) informed the plausibility form but nothing is fitted.
11. Flows below 0.5 veh/h are dropped (max 4,000 flows) and totals renormalized.

Results (veh/h, weekday; surface gate-to-gate "through" / local / freeway-ramp trips): AM 12,398 = 8,277 / 1,302 / 2,818 (23% ramp); midday 8,371 = 5,444 / 837 / 2,090 (25%); PM 12,819 = 8,030 / 1,470 / 3,319 (26%), of which 481 PM veh/h have a local end. Freeway gates PM: gate 422 out 1,034, gate 243 out 656, gate 418 in 936, gate 417 in 693. PM freeway-origin trips by destination sector (from the off-ramps): NE 40%, N 30%, E 15%, SE 8%, other 7%; ~3% end on College Ave gates. The through share, the ramp share and the destination mix are consequences of the assumptions above and are uncalibrated, so compare them against `data/calibration/` before trusting them. Run the tool for the ramp list, per-freeway-gate volumes and the per-gate target table.

## Calibration changes (baseline-calibration tasks 3.x)

Full before/after table: `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md`.

- BUGFIX: a two-way street appearing as an enter-only plus an exit-only gate (same street name, within 250 m) now splits the street volume by direction instead of giving each the full volume. A truly one-way gate keeps the whole volume.
- ASSUMPTION: `classDefaultAdt` residential 800 -> 300 and tertiary 5000 -> 3000 (hillside collectors; counted Alcatraz is not representative).
- ASSUMPTION: `assumedGateScale` = 0.3, one factor on all gates without a count (class-default ADT). Count-based gates and ramp volumes are unscaled. It brings the PM peak close to the simulated network's capacity; it is a stability workaround and not an estimate.
- ASSUMPTION: `ramps.localShare` 0.15 -> 0.30.
- DATA unchanged: Telegraph/Alcatraz TMC legs, Claremont override, ramp ADT.
