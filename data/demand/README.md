## Sources checked and not usable

- Oakland Socrata catalogue (`data.oaklandca.gov`, queries: traffic count, traffic volume, ADT, speed survey, counts, crash): no ADT, segment volume or speed-survey dataset exists; the only counts are the annual multi-modal turning-movement counts above (36 intersections, mostly outside the area).
- Berkeley open data (`data.cityofberkeley.info`, searched again on 2026-11-12 with the domain-restricted Socrata catalogue for traffic, count, volume, AADT, bicycle, speed, street, transportation): only signals, conduit, a street centerline layer (address ranges, no volumes), business and census tables. No traffic, turning-movement or bicycle count dataset exists there.
- Alameda CTC: the Oakland count zip contains `_ACTC_BikePedCounts_OaklandLocs.zip` (bicycle and pedestrian counts at Oakland locations); not read, because it holds no vehicle counts. No Alameda CTC count of motor vehicles on Berkeley or Oakland streets was found. MTC, Replica public summaries and Oakland speed surveys were not searched in depth.
- Oakland multimodal zip, intersections newly in range: Telegraph/40th, West/40th and Market/55th are used (PM counts, four dates each). Tunnel Rd & Caldecott Ln has only midday and Saturday counts (2012 to 2021), so it is not used for the PM peak. Broadway/51st, Shattuck, MLK Jr Way and Ashby have no Oakland count in the zip.
- Caltrans Traffic_AADT ArcGIS layer and GIS service list: no ramp-volume layer exists in the services listed (CHhighway folder); ramp ADT is only published as the District workbooks above, so those are used.
- Caltrans AADT on SR-13 at Claremont Ave (PM 10.79, AADT 32,500 back / 23,500 ahead, 2023-24 count year) counts SR-13, which is Ashby Ave and Tunnel Road. The ahead figure belongs to Ashby Ave west of Claremont Ave and is a station on that block. Until the sixteenth calibration round it was applied to Claremont Ave with a factor of 0.6 (see step 2).

## Method

1. Gate peak volume. DATA: a surface gate takes PM peak-hour entering/leaving volumes from the leg of the nearest counted intersection whose leg street and compass direction match the gate's street and bearing (within 2.5 km). Four intersections are counted (Telegraph/Alcatraz, Telegraph/40th, West/40th, Market/55th; mean of 4 counts each), so the Telegraph gate on the south edge uses the north leg at 40th St (107 m away) and the north-edge gate the north leg at Alcatraz Ave (1.15 km away); the 40th St gates at the south-west corner use the 40th St legs at West St, some more than 1 km away. The distance is printed per gate in the build log. If several gate nodes share a leg the volume is split among them by whether they can emit/absorb traffic.
2. ASSUMPTION: the Claremont Ave gate north of Ashby Ave has no count and takes the secondary class default (9,000 ADT). It used to take the SR-13 AADT ([CaltransAADT]) of 23,500 x 0.6 = 14,100 ADT, but that record counts Ashby Ave. The station that replaced the gate sat on a block most of the gate's traffic does not use, so the fit offered 1,136 veh/h leaving at the gate in the PM peak against a target of 698, through the all-way stop at Russell St (sixteenth round of the calibration record).
3. DATA: Caltrans AADT stations on the gate's street, nearest station to the gate (two-way ADT of the side of the station the gate lies on): Ashby Ave west gate 28,000 (SR-13 at the SR-123 junction, PM 13.178, east side); Tunnel Road gates 27,000 (SR-13 at Domingo Ave, PM 10.69, east side); San Pablo Ave gates 21,700 (SR-123 at JCT RTE 13) or 20,500 (SR-123 at Stanford Ave, mean of 21,300 and 19,700). The count year is the 2023-24 vintage noted in [CaltransAADT]. ASSUMPTION: all other surface gates use per-class default ADT (primary 14,000; secondary 9,000; tertiary 5,000; residential 800; service 150), as documented, except that a class with at least three counted streets takes the median of their ADT (`classDefaultFromCounts`, see "Sixth round" below). Gates of one street that lie within 250 m of each other (a street split in two ways at the edge) share the street volume instead of each taking all of it; this includes the two Tunnel Road gates and the two Claremont Ave gates. Freeway gates are handled in steps 8 to 10.
4. ASSUMPTION: peak hour = ADT x K (K = 0.09, urban arterial range 0.08-0.10; K and D definitions in [FHWA-TMG2022], see also [HCM7]; the 0.08-0.10 range itself is not source-verified in `docs/references.md`); directional inbound share 0.45 (PM). Count-based gates use the measured split.
5. ASSUMPTION: AM and midday gate volumes = PM peak-hour volume x 0.85 and 0.62; inbound shares 0.58 (AM), 0.50 (midday), 0.45 (PM). Uncalibrated.
6. DATA + ASSUMPTION: local trips (LODES per [LODES]; trip-rate conventions per [ITE-TGM11] are not used; the rates below are assumptions). LODES resident workers and jobs per block map to the nearest node on a residential link anywhere in the model area (the whole network, not only the study area; 702 distinct nodes). ASSUMPTION rates: 3.2 daily trips per resident worker, 3.5 per job, vehicle mode share 0.62, peak-hour share 0.085/0.06/0.095 (AM/midday/PM), origin share of resident trips 0.7/0.5/0.3, 80% of local trips external (a trip with an end in the wider area is external to its block, so it goes to a gate). DATA: each block's external trips are split by LODES home/work direction sector, then to gates by angular proximity (Gaussian, sigma 45 deg) x gate in/out target. Remaining 20% are internal, gravity weighted by attraction x exp(-d/1 km) (ASSUMPTION). Local use of any gate is capped at 50% of its target.
7. ASSUMPTION: through trips (gate to gate). Residual gate inflow/outflow after local trips is balanced by Furness/IPF ([DemingStephan1940]; [Furness1965] is UNVERIFIED; successive-approximation relative [Fratar1954]) (outflow targets scaled to total inflow). Seed = target outflow x turn factor: straight-through (>=135 deg) 1.0, adjacent (75-135 deg) 0.6, oblique 0.3, U-turn-like (<40 deg) 0.05; x3 for same-street pairs. This gravity/turn-plausibility form is a modeling assumption and is not fitted.
8. Freeway ramps. Topology (generic, no area-specific code): freeway-class links (`config.freewayClasses`, here `motorway`, including the former `motorway_link` ramps) form the freeway; a ramp is a surface node touched by both freeway and surface links. Off-ramp = a freeway link arrives; on-ramp = a freeway link leaves. The mainline gates that feed an off-ramp (or take an on-ramp) are the pure-source (pure-sink) freeway gates reached walking upstream (downstream) along freeway links. The Rockridge extract has 15 freeway gates (SR-24 south-west end, several carriageways; SR-24 east end toward the Caldecott Tunnel; SR-13 south-east end) and 36 ramp connections at 32 surface nodes: SR-24 at MLK Jr Way (one off, two on), 52nd St (off, on), Telegraph Ave (off at Aileen St, on at 56th St), Claremont Ave (off at Clifton St, on at Hudson St), Keith Ave, Miles Ave, Brookside Ave and Broadway (College/Broadway/Patton area), Caldecott Lane, the SR-13 terminals at Tunnel Road and Broadway Terrace, and hillside connectors at Glenwood Glade and Pinewood Road.
9. DATA + ASSUMPTION: ramp volumes. DATA: ramp ADT from [CaltransRampADT] for SR-24 and SR-13, matched to each network ramp by street name (`streetMatch`) and type (the description contains OFF or ON), or by an explicit alias where the surface street differs from the Caltrans description (`ramps.aliases`, an ASSUMPTION based on OSM destination tags, postmile order and location; Keith Ave = EB OFF TO BROADWAY, Miles Ave = WB ON FR PATTON ST and WB OFF TO COLLEGE AVE, Brookside Ave and Broadway at Brookside = EB ON FR BROADWAY, Broadway at Patton = WB OFF TO BROADWAY, Caldecott Lane = the Caldecott Ln rows). `SEG` (segment) rows count when they name exactly one street; combined rows for MLK Jr Way and 52nd St (PM R2.088, R2.100, R2.501, R2.558) are excluded because the per-street SEG rows (2012 and 2016-17) cover the same ramps and would double count. Rows of one street and type are summed and shared by the ramps of that group; ramps that alias to the same Caltrans row share it (before the fourth round each took the full row, which counted EB ON FR BROADWAY twice, 7,800 ADT). Latest count year per row; no growth applied (ASSUMPTION). The 52nd St and MLK Jr Way ramps carry much more than the four ramps of the earlier model: EB off 14,200 (52nd) and 18,400 (MLK), WB on 12,900 (52nd) and 19,300 (MLK, split over two on-ramps). Peak hour = ADT x 0.09 (ASSUMPTION, as in step 4) x period volume factor (step 5). 22 of 36 ramp connections have a published count. The other 14 (the three SR-13 terminals at Tunnel Road, Glenwood Glade and Pinewood Road, and seven Broadway connections east of Tunnel Road) use `ramps.defaultAdt` = 500 (ASSUMPTION; the SR-13/SR-24 connector rows R4.8 to R5.3 describe freeway-to-freeway movements and are not matched to surface ramps). Mainline SR-24 and SR-13 AADT (134,000 to 164,000 and 75,000 to 79,000, [CaltransAADT]) is context only: freeway-mainline-to-mainline trips never touch surface streets and are not generated.
10. Ramp demand sits at the freeway mainline gates, not at ramps. A trip that uses a ramp has the mainline gate as origin (off-ramp traffic) or destination (on-ramp traffic) and a surface gate or an in-area node as the other end; the simulator's router picks the ramp. Earlier, each flow was generated per (ramp, gate) pair, with the destination weights computed from that one ramp's bearing, which suited four ramps with one natural route each. With ramps at 52nd St, MLK Jr Way, Telegraph, Claremont, Broadway and elsewhere, a driver can choose between them, so the ramp is no longer fixed in the demand. The volume of a mainline gate is the sum of the ADT shares of the ramps it can reach (a ramp's ADT is split equally among its feeding or receiving mainline gates, so the ramp totals add up). ASSUMPTION: the choice of surface end uses direction plausibility exp(-(dTheta/90 deg)^2), floor 0.03, with dTheta the angle between the bearing ramp to destination and the bearing ramp to study-area center, averaged over the gate's ramps weighted by their volume (no distance term). Local ends take a share `ramps.localShare` = 0.60 (0.30 until the fourth round); a surface gate takes at most 40% of its target as ramp trips (`ramps.maxShareOfGate`, water-filling). Ramp trips count toward the surface gate flows, are subtracted from the residual that the Furness step (step 7) balances, and ramp trips with a local end replace part of that block's external local trips. The model's ramp volumes are consequences of routing, not inputs, so compare them with the Caltrans ramp ADT in the calibration step.
11. Car flows are cut to about `maxFlows` = 8,000 by thinning: a flow at or above a threshold t stays, a smaller flow v is kept with probability v/t (seeded) and then carries t, so the expected volume of every origin and destination is unchanged. Iterative proportional fitting on the kept pairs [DemingStephan1940] then restores each origin's and destination's volume within its class (ramp, gate to gate, local), and one factor per class restores the class total. The earlier cut kept the largest flows and renormalized with a single factor, which inflated ramp trips by 23%. With 143 surface gates, 702 local nodes and 15 mainline gates the full table has about 700,000 candidate flows per period.

Results (veh/h, weekday; surface gate-to-gate "through" / local (a gate or a block at one end, or a block to a block) / mainline-gate "ramp" trips), sixth-round model area and settings (`ramps.localShare` 0.60, class defaults from counts, peripheral rules): AM 22,121 = 8,674 / 2,340 / 11,107; midday 17,333 = 7,714 / 1,517 / 8,102; PM 27,393 = 12,044 / 2,282 / 13,068, of which 7,787 PM veh/h of ramp trips have a local end. In the fourth round (`assumedGateScale` 0.5) the PM table was 24,924 = 8,572 / 3,285 / 13,068. The same table at the start of the fourth round (`assumedGateScale` 1.0, local share 0.30, before the accounting fixes): PM 28,759 = 9,827 / 1,944 / 16,988. At the freeway mainline gates in the PM, 6,703 veh/h enter the model (off-ramp trips) and 6,365 leave it (on-ramp trips). The sum of ramp ADT x K over the 36 ramps is 6,786 off and 6,282 on. Of the PM freeway-origin trips, 59% end at a block inside the area, and by compass sector from the off-ramps W takes 46%, NW 22% and SW 16%. The build log prints a gate accounting table (target and modeled inflow and outflow per gate kind, and the ratio at each counted gate) and the ramp totals; run it after every change to the demand method. The through share, the ramp share and the destination mix are consequences of the assumptions above and are uncalibrated.

## Walking and biking demand (multimodal-agents design D6)

Flows with `mode: 'pedestrian'` or `mode: 'bicycle'` are appended to each period's flow list after the car flows. Car flows have no `mode` field. The volume field is still called `vehPerHour`; for these modes it means persons per hour. Code: `tools/lib/demand-active.ts`, settings: `active` in `data/demand/rockridge.config.json`.

DATA: PM peak-hour pedestrian crosswalk volumes and bicycle movement volumes at Telegraph Ave & Alcatraz Ave in the four Oakland count reports already in `data/demand/raw/oakland-multimodal/` (mean of the four: about 151 people on foot and 82 on bicycles; the 2021 report is lower than the earlier years). Each mode gets one scaling factor so that the modeled PM peak-hour people passing that intersection on shortest paths (trip ends included) equal the observed mean. The factor applies to all periods and places. A pedestrian passing through counts as one crossing, which slightly undercounts turning trips. Nothing else is counted: the spatial pattern away from that intersection is not validated, and the model has not been calibrated against walking or biking counts.

ASSUMPTION: everything else, all labeled in the config.

1. Person trips per resident worker and per job use the car model's rates (`local.*`) and its origin and destination shares by period. Mode share: 6% walking, 4% bicycle. These only set the prior level; the count scaling then fixes the level (wider area, prior at the counted intersection, PM peak: 309 pedestrians and 776 bicycles per hour, against 151 and 82 observed, so factors of 0.49 and 0.20 with a clamp at 0.2; the sixth round removed the clamp, see below).
2. Pedestrians. Trips start at the LODES-based nodes (now anywhere in the model area). 20% go to or from gates within 800 m of network distance; of the rest, 35% end on a primary or secondary street inside the study area (weighted by jobs within 200 m), and the remainder go to other in-area nodes. Destination weight falls off exponentially with network distance (mean 500 m, longest walk 1,200 m). Motorway links are not walkable.
3. Bicycles. 45% of trips from in-area nodes go to or from gates (within 4 km, mean trip 2 km), the rest to other in-area nodes. Gate-to-gate through trips equal 50% of the in-area bicycle trips; a gate pair's weight is higher when its shortest path follows `bikeLane` and `bikeRoute` links (up to four times). Paths use a cost of 0.8 on bike-lane links and 0.9 on bike-route links.
4. Period profile relative to the PM peak: pedestrians 0.75 (AM), 0.9 (midday); bicycles 0.9 (AM), 0.5 (midday).
5. Flows under 0.05 persons per hour are dropped and the list is capped at 2,000 pedestrian and 1,500 bicycle flows per period (totals preserved). With cars (capped at 8,000), a period holds under 12,000 flows (PM 10,986 including 2,000 pedestrian and 1,500 bicycle flows; person volumes AM / midday / PM: pedestrians 2,388 / 2,816 / 3,075 per hour, bicycles 1,479 / 812 / 1,605 per hour). The commercial-street rule (35% of walking trips end on a primary or secondary street inside the study area) is unchanged.

Sources checked: the ACS commute mode-share tables (B08301) would give walking and bicycle shares for the LODES tracts, but the Census API returned "Missing Key" without an API key and no token was available, so mode shares are assumptions. City of Berkeley and Alameda County bicycle and pedestrian counts were not searched in depth.

## Calibration changes (baseline-calibration tasks 3.x)

Full before/after table: `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md`.

- BUGFIX: a two-way street appearing as an enter-only plus an exit-only gate (same street name, within 250 m) now splits the street volume by direction instead of giving each the full volume. A truly one-way gate keeps the whole volume.
- ASSUMPTION: `classDefaultAdt` residential 800 -> 300 and tertiary 5000 -> 3000 (hillside collectors; counted Alcatraz is not representative).
- ASSUMPTION: `assumedGateScale` = 0.35 (0.3 until the second round), one factor on all gates without a count (class-default ADT). Count-based gates and ramp volumes are unscaled. After junction joining and the re-route change the network takes about 8k veh/h offered, and 0.35 is the highest value that keeps PM backlog under 2% and arterial speed above 4 m/s; 0.7 gives 124 gridlock removals. It is a stability workaround and not an estimate.
- ASSUMPTION: `ramps.localShare` 0.15 -> 0.30.
- DATA unchanged: Telegraph/Alcatraz TMC legs, Claremont override, ramp ADT.
- Second round (record steps G to J): the study area is derived from five ordered boundary streets, graph nodes of one real intersection are joined by the importer (`junctionJoinRadius`), and the simulator's re-route share is 80% per 60 s epoch. These change the network and route choice, not the demand tables; the demand was regenerated because gate and node ids moved. `ramps.localShare` stays 0.30 (0.45 and 0.6 were tested and moved the headline shares less than the seed spread).
- Third round (record steps K to O): signals, the ramp-queue rule and the checks of the re-route share and `assumedGateScale` changed the simulator, not the demand tables; `assumedGateScale` stays 0.35 and `ramps.localShare` 0.30. ## Wider model area (baseline-calibration task 7.1)

The model area grew from 0.016 x 0.019 degrees to 0.066 x 0.0305 degrees (design D6). What changed in the demand step:

- LODES blocks, Caltrans AADT and ramp ADT are fetched for the new bbox (`fetch-demand.ts --force`); three more Oakland intersections are read.
- Gates now sit on Ashby Ave, Sacramento St, Shattuck Ave, MLK Jr Way, Adeline St, San Pablo Ave, Tunnel Road, Broadway and the freeway mainlines; the Caltrans stations on SR-13 and SR-123 give the volumes of five of them as DATA.

The 0.35 and the 300 / 3,000 cuts had been stability workarounds for gates a few hundred meters from the study area. The fourth round (below) set `assumedGateScale` to 0.5, again as a stability workaround; the first untuned run is below.
- Ramp demand is expressed at mainline gates (step 10), and unmatched ramps no longer default to 3,000.
- Local trips start and end anywhere in the area, not only in the study area.

## Sixth round: audit, peripheral trips, class defaults, balancing (baseline-calibration task 9)

`tools/build-demand.ts` now writes `data/demand/<area>.audit.md` on every run: for every surface gate its side, street, class, basis (DATA or ASSUMPTION), daily volume used, peak target and modeled in and out, lane capacity and the share of its trips that come within 400 m of the study area; the offered car trips of each period split into three relevance classes (the free-flow route of the trip uses the study area or a bounding street, comes within 400 m of it, or never does); and the cordon table that compares the modeled flow at each side of the model with the lanes there times the measured signalized capacity (837 veh/h per lane, validation cases b1 and b2; 2,184 for a freeway lane, case i1). Routes are free-flow shortest paths on the network file (`tools/lib/demand-audit.ts`). The audit applies to the final flow list, so it shows the effect of every rule below.

- Peripheral trips (config `peripheral`, ASSUMPTION). Gate-to-gate trips that never come near the study area are kept as background load, with two rules. P1 drops corner-clipping pairs, which enter and leave through gates less than 600 m apart along the model perimeter or whose route stays within 300 m of the model edge. Their volume leaves the gates' totals, counted gates included; the build log says how much (PM peak 606 veh/h on the current network, 1,642 on the earlier one). The earlier alternative, refitting the gate totals without those pairs, moved the same volume onto longer pairs and made the PM peak complete 7% fewer trips (record step S2). P2 caps a gate's modeled volume at min(target, lane capacity x 837); the excess comes from that gate's peripheral through trips only. It removed nothing, because no gate direction exceeds its lane capacity (the highest, Telegraph Ave south outbound, is at 0.86). Ramp volumes are never reduced.
- Class defaults (config `classDefaultFromCounts`, DATA-derived). A class with at least three counted streets takes the median of their ADT instead of the documented value, where a counted street's ADT is a TMC leg's peak-hour entering plus leaving volume over K, or a Caltrans station ADT times its factor (one value per street). Counted streets are the busiest of their class, so the median is a high estimate for gates without a count. On the current network only tertiary qualifies (Alcatraz Ave 12,992, Market St 11,497, West St 5,289, Tunnel Road 27,000; median 12,200 against 5,000). Primary has two counted streets, secondary two (Telegraph Ave, Claremont Ave) and residential one, so their documented values stay. `assumedGateScale` is gone: it was a stability workaround and is 1.0 by construction.
- Balancing (config `gravity.balanceKeepsCounted`). After local and ramp trips are subtracted, the through inflow supply and outflow demand differ (PM: 12,649 supply against 14,478 demand on the current network). Counted gates now keep both their inflow and outflow and the assumed gates are scaled to close the gap, on whichever side has the surplus. Before, every gate's outflow was scaled to 0.77 of its target in the PM peak. A check found no double counting: the modeled inflow at a counted gate, summed over through, local and ramp trips, was 1.01 to 1.03 of its target before this change.
- Gates and LODES. LODES blocks outside the model `bbox` are dropped (the raw file was cut for the larger box).
- Walking and biking count scaling has no clamp. The factor makes the persons passing the counted intersection in the final flow list (after the flow cap) equal the count: pedestrians 0.355 (310 before scaling, 151 observed), bicycles 0.161 (819 before scaling, 82 observed; the 1,500-flow cap removes small flows that pass the node, which is why 0.161 and not 0.100). ## Count-constrained demand (seventh round, baseline-calibration task 10)

After the sixth round the simulator's capacities sat in published ranges, yet at full demand the peaks completed about 14,000 of 24,500 offered trips an hour. The builder fitted gate volumes to counts but never checked what the trips load onto links inside the model. This section adds that check as steps 12 to 16 of the method. They run after step 11 (the flow list) and change only car flows; walking and biking flows are untouched. Code: `tools/lib/assign.ts` (assignment), `tools/lib/fit-counts.ts` (adjustment and guard), `tools/lib/demand-fit.ts` (stations, orchestration, report); settings: `countFit` in `rockridge.config.json`; generated report: `rockridge.fit.md`.

12. Static assignment (ASSUMPTION: free-flow routes; the cost model is copied from the simulator). Every car flow goes to its shortest path over link states with the simulator router's cost at free flow: link time (length over speed limit, plus 3 s per speed hump) times 1.25 on residential and service links, plus per movement a turn penalty (right 1 s, left 4 s, U-turn 25 s), a control delay (signal 10 s, all-way stop 6 s, minor approach at a two-way stop 5 s, circle 3 s) and the traversal time of the movement. One-ways come from the link directions, OSM turn bans apply for the period they name, and U-turns are allowed only at dead ends, as in `crates/sim/src/eff.rs`. Approximations: the simulator multiplies each driver's cost-to-go by a seeded factor of up to +/-8%, while the builder splits every flow equally over 5 assignments whose link costs carry such a factor (`draws`, `noise`); the signal and stop delays are the fixed free-flow constants, not the congested ones. The step adds about 6 s to the build for the three periods.
13. Count stations as link constraints (DATA, with the K and directional factors of steps 4 and 5 for the AM and midday targets, ASSUMPTION). 69 stations per period: (a) 25 gate entries and exits at the 15 counted gates (TMC legs and Caltrans stations by street and direction), each as the trips generated at (entering) or absorbed at (leaving) the gate node, which is the volume on its links without counting trips that pass through the node; (b) 16 turning-movement leg volumes at the two counted intersections inside the model (Telegraph Ave and Alcatraz Ave, Market St and 55th St; entering and leaving on each leg; Telegraph Ave and 40th St and West St and 40th St lie 300 to 400 m outside it, so only their gates represent them); (c) 6 directional Caltrans AADT volumes at the three stations that lie inside the model (Tunnel Road, Ashby Ave west of Claremont Ave since the sixteenth round, and San Pablo Ave at Stanford Ave), as the link of the street nearest the station in each direction, with ADT x factor x K x period volume x the period's inbound share; the two stations outside the model (Ashby Ave and San Pablo Ave at SR-13) are represented by their gates; (d) 22 ramps with a published ADT, as the volume on the freeway-class link at the ramp's surface node (ADT x K x period volume). Ramps on the default ADT of 500 are not constrained. Until the sixteenth round the third station sat on Claremont Ave with the 0.6 attenuation of step 2.
14. Matrix adjustment (the method family is count-based OD matrix estimation, [VanZuylen1980] and [Willumsen1981]; the update below is our implementation and was not checked against those papers' equations). Each iteration multiplies flow k by exp of the weighted mean, over the counted links on its paths (weight = station weight x share of the flow crossing it), of log(target / assigned), then clamps its cumulative factor to 0.1 to 3 (ramp flows 0.1 to 10: the free-flow router sends most trips toward one ramp, so reaching the published ramp ADT elsewhere needs larger factors). Ramp stations have weight 2, others 1. The stopping rule is in step 17 (the eighth round replaced the iteration limit of 400 as the usual exit). Flows that cross no counted link (about a sixth of the flows) take the volume-weighted median factor of the counted flows of their type (through, local, ramp), `countFit.uncounted`; the alternatives are the median over all types and 1. ASSUMPTION: the bounds, the weights, the 5-draw assignment, and the rule for uncounted flows. DATA: the targets.
15. Capacity guard (ASSUMPTION, not data). An uncounted link may carry at most 110% of its capacity. Capacity comes from the validation suite's measured values (`countFit.capacityGuard.capacity`, each entry names its case): 837 veh/h per lane at a signal (b1, b2), 473 per approach at an all-way stop (d1), 574 per minor approach at a two-way stop (e4), 1,656 per lane on a free link (g1), 804 per approach at a circle (f1), 2,184 per lane on a motorway (i1) and 500 per lane at a signal terminal of a ramp (i2). Links on a count are exempt. The guard works inside the fit as soft upper limits with weight 0.5, so a count that conflicts with a limit wins; flows that touch no count then follow the limits exactly. The cases measure one primitive on a tiny network, so a link-level limit ignores turning conflicts at the junction.
16. Report (`rockridge.fit.md`): offered trips by type before the fit, after it and after the guard, per-flow factor percentiles, flows at the bounds, stations no flow crosses, the station table for every period (target, assigned before, after, final, ratio, tolerance), the station fit by family, the links that bound the guard, and notes on stations that were skipped.

Result (veh/h, PM peak, car trips): offered by the build 25,462 (through 10,113, local 2,282, ramp 13,068); after the count fit 24,891; after the guard 23,524 (through 8,289, local 2,096, ramp 13,140). AM 20,602 to 20,341; midday 16,010 to 15,282. Stations within 15% of their target: 28 of 69 before the fit, 66 of 69 after, in every period; ramp totals at counted ramps are 1.00 (off) and 1.01 (on) of ADT x K. About 2,200 of 7,900 PM flows end at the lower bound and 460 at the upper bound, so the counts conflict with the assumed trip table in many places: the free-flow router sends many ramp trips to a few ramps, which the factors then cut. Gates that stayed outside 10% in the seventh round: the two Tunnel Road gates (entering 0.69 and 0.68 of the count in the PM peak). Each gate already took half the Caltrans count; the cause is the Caldecott Lane on-ramp, see step 17. The on-ramp at node 1745 (Caldecott Lane, 61 veh/h) stays at 2.2 times its count: the flows that use it already sit at the lower bound of 0.1.

## Eighth round: convergence, route shares, restrained assignment, ramp pools (baseline-calibration task 11)

Steps 17 to 21 change the fit of steps 12 to 16 and the ramp trips of step 9. Code: `tools/lib/fit-counts.ts`, `tools/lib/share-targets.ts`, `tools/lib/assign.ts`, `tools/lib/demand-fit.ts`, `tools/build-demand.ts`; settings: `countFit.assignment`, `countFit.shares`, `ramps.destination.pooling` and `ramps.destination.accessTauS`; record: `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md`, eighth round. Setting `FIT_DUMP=<file>` when running `build-demand.ts` writes the fit's inputs (flows, types, stations) as JSON for analysis.

17. Stopping rule and conflicts (ASSUMPTION: tolerance 1%, stall 0.2% in 25 iterations, 80% on the bound, `countFit.tol`, `stallWindow`, `stallGain`, `blockedShare`). The loop stops when every count station that can still move is within `tol` (log error) and the volume-weighted mean relative step of the flows is under `softStep`; as stalled when the worst error of those stations improves by less than `stallGain` over `stallWindow` iterations; or when no flow moves by more than `stationary`. A station is set aside, and listed with the stations that oppose it, when at least `blockedShare` of its volume sits on the bound it would need to move, or when the volume of the stations that pull its flows the other way plus the flows the capacity guard pulls down covers half of its shortfall (checked every 10 iterations from the 20th). The report gives iterations, the reason, the worst error with and without the set-aside stations, and the table "Stations that cannot be met". The update is unchanged (geometric mean, damping 1): over-relaxation oscillates. Gates of one street that take halves of one count (same source text, within 250 m) are one station on the group's trips.
18. Route shares as ratio constraints (DATA targets, ASSUMPTION weight 0.5). The targets file gives, for each select-link block, the share of trips from (to) the freeway, and for the Claremont ramps the share of north-side trips (`freewayRamp.side`) that use each named street, with qualifiers such as "north of Alcatraz Avenue" resolved to the street's links on that side. Select link: (volume on the block from trips with a freeway end) / (volume on the block); ramp: (volume that uses the ramp and the street) / (volume that uses the ramp), the joint use taken as the smaller of the two path shares. A freeway end is a node with a motorway-class link, as in the simulator's report. In the update the numerator flows get (target / modeled)^w and the rest of the denominator ((1 - target) / (1 - modeled))^w in the same weighted geometric mean as the counts, with weight 0.5 against 1 to 2 for the counts because the shares come from a device sample. AM and midday use the same shares as the PM peak (the PM-peak variant of a share applies in the PM period only), ASSUMPTION.
19. Assignment by successive averages (`countFit.assignment`, ASSUMPTION: 10 passes x 2 walks, alpha 0.15, beta 4, noise 8%, 2 rounds). Each pass computes the cost-to-go to every destination over link costs times the BPR factor 1 + alpha (volume / capacity)^beta ([Spiess1990] for the form, which the values of alpha and beta are not taken from), then each flow is loaded with driver walks that follow the simulator's rule (least movement cost + cost-to-go times a factor 1 +/- noise per candidate link; a walk that loops is replaced by the shortest path). Link volumes are the running average of the pass volumes ([PowellSheffi1982]). Capacities are the capacity guard's table. In the second round the table is refitted and reassigned at its own volumes. `method: "free"` keeps the free-flow assignment of step 12.
20. Ramp trips per ramp (ASSUMPTION: access decay 300 s). In step 9 each ramp trip's surface end is drawn from the ramp's own volume, bearing and an exp(-t / 300 s) decay in the router's cost from the surface end to the ramp, and sent to the mainline gate(s) the ramp reaches. `pooling: "gate"` restores the earlier draw over every ramp a gate reaches. Until round nine the two west-end westbound stubs of SR-24 were separate gates, so a trip to one could not use the other's ramps; the importer now merges freeway-edge stubs (step 22), and the per-ramp pools stay because the merged gate reaches 9 to 10 ramps up to 4.5 km apart.
21. Report additions (`rockridge.fit.md`): the route-share table (target, offered, count-only fit, final), the table of stations that cannot be met, the assignment settings.

Result (PM peak, car trips): offered 25,783 by the build, 25,101 after the count fit, 24,078 after the guard (the count-only column of the report is the fit without the shares and the guard). 66 stations (the Tunnel Road, San Pablo Ave and 43rd St gate pairs are one station each); stopped by tolerance after 114 iterations (AM 117, midday 96); 63 of 66 within 15%, the other three in conflict (Caldecott Lane on-ramp +117%, Tunnel Road gate pair -26%, Market St/55th St west leg entering +24%). 2,742 PM flows (7,959 of the 24,230 veh/h offered on counted flows) end at the lower bound; they average 2.9 veh/h. The 15 route shares end within 5 points of their targets in the static assignment (the simulator sees them differently; see the record).

## Eleventh round: hold-out, implied volumes, damped cost loop (baseline-calibration task 14)

26. Hold-out stations (`countFit.validation`, decision D17; [WebTAG-M3.1] C.3.2 and 4.2.1, audit C2). Each station gets a corridor: the street of a gate, the intersection of a turning-movement count, the street of a Caltrans station, and all freeway ramps together (9 corridors on Rockridge). Within a corridor the stations are ordered by size (ties by id) and every fourth one, starting at the second, is validation-only; a corridor with one station gives it up, so every corridor is covered. No random draw. The build fits twice: first without the validation stations (the out-of-sample figures in the fit report, "Held-out stations", and a demand file for a simulator run, `countFit.validation.demandOutput`, kept under `target/`), then on every count (the demand that ships; leaving a quarter of the counts out of the shipped fit lowered the PM demand by 19% in a trial (23,666 to 19,243 veh/h) because uncounted flows fall to the median factor). The station list with corridors, held flags and link indices is written to `data/demand/<area>.stations.json` for the calibration report.
27. GEH and size bands in the fit report. Every station row has its GEH and whether it passes GEH below 5 and the size band (within 100 veh/h below 700, within 15% from 700 to 2,700, within 400 above), and each period reports the share of fitted and held-out stations passing. Those figures are the static assignment against the counts; the simulator's own flows are scored by `tools/assess.ts` (see `data/calibration/README.md`).
28. Implied volumes (`countFit.implied`, DERIVED: between DATA and ASSUMPTION). No count exists on Hillegass Ave or Colby St, but two published numbers imply a volume: the Claremont Ave ramp count (DATA, Caltrans ramp ADT times K) times the street's share of the ramp trips from (to) the north (DATA, resident-provided select-link source) is the freeway-related volume on the street, and dividing by the share of the street's traffic that is freeway-related gives the total. The file does not say whether the ramp via shares are of all ramp trips or of the trips from the north side; the build computes both readings (A: all; B: the north fraction of the ramp's offered trips, 87% to 89%) and the soft target is their midpoint, with the range in the report. Only the named ramps feed the numerator, so the totals are lower bounds if other ramps feed the street. The target is a volume constraint on the longest link of the select-link block, weight 0.5 (below the counts, as for the shares), and the fit report prints the arithmetic and the modeled volume before and after. Martin St and Benvenue Ave have a ramp share but no freeway-share of their own traffic, so no volume is implied for them.
29. Damped cost loop (`tools/cost-loop.ts`, decision D18; audit R1). The ninth round's loop moved trips toward links that were fast in the last run, which the next run then loaded (share error 12.6, 9.9, 15.1 points, stability lost). The loop now averages the cost vector across iterations by the method of successive averages (weight 1/n), takes each iteration's costs as flow-weighted means over several seeds (PM: the ten scoring seeds, other periods: three), reports the relative gap and the share of links whose cost changed by under 1%, and stops on a relative gap under 0.10, on the iteration limit, or when the PM peak stops being stable. It scores each iteration and keeps one only if it improves the headline-share error by at least 0.5 points while the PM peak stays stable under D16 and the GEH shares do not fall by more than 5 points; otherwise it removes the delays file and rebuilds the unchanged demand. Run: `npm run cost-loop` (skips with a message when `target/release/calibrate` is missing).
30. Result of the first run of the damped loop (record, eleventh round): the cost vector converged (relative gap 0.27, 0.15, 0.08), the headline-share error stayed between 9.5 and 12.2 points (10.9 at iteration 0) and the PM peak stayed unstable (2.5 to 5.7 removals per hour), so no iteration was kept and `rockridge.linkdelays.json` was removed. The shipped demand uses free-flow and BPR costs, the implied volumes of step 28 and the all-count fit of step 26.

51. Assumed movement flows (`assumedMovementFlows`, ASSUMPTION; owner decision of 2026-11-14 to force the count). The one movement so far is the Colby St northbound left turn onto Alcatraz Ave westbound: 131 veh/h counted in the PM peak (78 AM; midday takes the PM count times 0.62 like every other count), 13 veh/h assigned before. Code: `tools/lib/assumed-movements.ts`, which holds no street names; the movement is named in the config the way the volunteer count file words it, and its volume per period is the station target of that movement. Rule: a flow of the seed table is a candidate when its route through the movement costs at most `maxDetour` (1.3, ASSUMPTION) times its best route at free flow, with the simulator router's cost, and the route does not loop; the volume is split over the candidates in proportion to their seed volume, `throughShare` of it over flows with no end in the study area and the rest over flows with an end inside. Flows under 0.5 veh/h are dropped and their volume shared by the others. The resulting flows carry `via`, the movement as an entry link and an exit link, and the fit holds their volume (`FitOptions.pinned`), so the other flows are fitted to the counts less the assumed ones. The assignment of the fit routes a `via` flow to the entry link, through the movement and on to its destination, the way the simulator does.
