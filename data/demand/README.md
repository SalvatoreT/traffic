# Demand data and method (Rockridge)

Regenerate everything: `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 npx tsx tools/fetch-demand.ts` (add `--force` to re-download; the env var is only needed behind an HTTP proxy), then `npx tsx tools/build-demand.ts` (run `tools/build-transit.ts` before it when a transit file should feed bus-stop walking trips). Settings: `data/demand/rockridge.config.json`. Raw files (gitignored, stored in R2) were retrieved 2026-11-11 and refetched for the wider model area on 2026-11-12. All volumes below are for the model area (`bbox` in `data/areas/rockridge.json`), which is much larger than the study area; see "Wider model area" at the end.

## Sources checked and not usable

- Oakland Socrata catalogue (`data.oaklandca.gov`, queries: traffic count, traffic volume, ADT, speed survey, counts, crash): no ADT, segment volume or speed-survey dataset exists; the only counts are the annual multi-modal turning-movement counts above (36 intersections, mostly outside the area).
- Berkeley open data (`data.cityofberkeley.info`, searched again on 2026-11-12 with the domain-restricted Socrata catalogue for traffic, count, volume, AADT, bicycle, speed, street, transportation): only signals, conduit, a street centerline layer (address ranges, no volumes), business and census tables. No traffic, turning-movement or bicycle count dataset exists there.
- Alameda CTC: the Oakland count zip contains `_ACTC_BikePedCounts_OaklandLocs.zip` (bicycle and pedestrian counts at Oakland locations); not read, because it holds no vehicle counts. No Alameda CTC count of motor vehicles on Berkeley or Oakland streets was found. MTC, Replica public summaries and Oakland speed surveys were not searched in depth.
- Oakland multimodal zip, intersections newly in range: Telegraph/40th, West/40th and Market/55th are used (PM counts, four dates each). Tunnel Rd & Caldecott Ln has only midday and Saturday counts (2012 to 2021), so it is not used for the PM peak. Broadway/51st, Shattuck, MLK Jr Way and Ashby have no Oakland count in the zip.
- Caltrans Traffic_AADT ArcGIS layer and GIS service list: no ramp-volume layer exists in the services listed (CHhighway folder); ramp ADT is only published as the District workbooks above, so those are used.
- Caltrans AADT on SR-13 Claremont Ave (PM 10.79, AADT 32,500 back / 23,500 ahead, 2023-24 count year) is the nearest state count to the Claremont/College gate but sits at Ashby/Tunnel Rd, so it is scaled (below).

## Method

1. Gate peak volume. DATA: a surface gate takes PM peak-hour entering/leaving volumes from the leg of the nearest counted intersection whose leg street and compass direction match the gate's street and bearing (within 2.5 km). Four intersections are counted (Telegraph/Alcatraz, Telegraph/40th, West/40th, Market/55th; mean of 4 counts each), so the Telegraph gate on the south edge uses the north leg at 40th St (107 m away) and the north-edge gate the north leg at Alcatraz Ave (1.15 km away); the 40th St gates at the south-west corner use the 40th St legs at West St, some more than 1 km away. The distance is printed per gate in the build log. If several gate nodes share a leg the volume is split among them by whether they can emit/absorb traffic.
2. DATA/ASSUMPTION: Claremont Ave gate: Caltrans SR-13 AADT ([CaltransAADT]) 23,500 x 0.6 (assumed attenuation) = 14,100 ADT, split between the two Claremont Ave gate nodes (see step 3).
3. DATA: Caltrans AADT stations on the gate's street, nearest station to the gate (two-way ADT of the side of the station the gate lies on): Ashby Ave west gate 28,000 (SR-13 at the SR-123 junction, PM 13.178, east side); Tunnel Road gates 27,000 (SR-13 at Domingo Ave, PM 10.69, east side); San Pablo Ave gates 21,700 (SR-123 at JCT RTE 13) or 20,500 (SR-123 at Stanford Ave, mean of 21,300 and 19,700). The count year is the 2023-24 vintage noted in [CaltransAADT]. ASSUMPTION: all other surface gates use per-class default ADT (primary 14,000; secondary 9,000; tertiary 5,000; residential 800; service 150), as documented, then multiplied by `assumedGateScale` (0.5, step T of the calibration record; the count-based gates and the ramps are not scaled). Gates of one street that lie within 250 m of each other (a street split in two ways at the edge) share the street volume instead of each taking all of it; this includes the two Tunnel Road gates and the two Claremont Ave gates. Freeway gates are handled in steps 8 to 10.
4. ASSUMPTION: peak hour = ADT x K (K = 0.09, urban arterial range 0.08-0.10; K and D definitions in [FHWA-TMG2022], see also [HCM7]; the 0.08-0.10 range itself is not source-verified in `docs/references.md`); directional inbound share 0.45 (PM). Count-based gates use the measured split.
5. ASSUMPTION: AM and midday gate volumes = PM peak-hour volume x 0.85 and 0.62; inbound shares 0.58 (AM), 0.50 (midday), 0.45 (PM). Uncalibrated.
6. DATA + ASSUMPTION: local trips (LODES per [LODES]; trip-rate conventions per [ITE-TGM11] are not used; the rates below are assumptions). LODES resident workers and jobs per block map to the nearest node on a residential link anywhere in the model area (the whole network, not only the study area; 702 distinct nodes). ASSUMPTION rates: 3.2 daily trips per resident worker, 3.5 per job, vehicle mode share 0.62, peak-hour share 0.085/0.06/0.095 (AM/midday/PM), origin share of resident trips 0.7/0.5/0.3, 80% of local trips external (a trip with an end in the wider area is external to its block, so it goes to a gate). DATA: each block's external trips are split by LODES home/work direction sector, then to gates by angular proximity (Gaussian, sigma 45 deg) x gate in/out target. Remaining 20% are internal, gravity weighted by attraction x exp(-d/1 km) (ASSUMPTION). Local use of any gate is capped at 50% of its target.
7. ASSUMPTION: through trips (gate to gate). Residual gate inflow/outflow after local trips is balanced by Furness/IPF ([DemingStephan1940]; [Furness1965] is UNVERIFIED; successive-approximation relative [Fratar1954]) (outflow targets scaled to total inflow). Seed = target outflow x turn factor: straight-through (>=135 deg) 1.0, adjacent (75-135 deg) 0.6, oblique 0.3, U-turn-like (<40 deg) 0.05; x3 for same-street pairs. This gravity/turn-plausibility form is a modeling assumption and is not fitted.
8. Freeway ramps. Topology (generic, no area-specific code): freeway-class links (`config.freewayClasses`, here `motorway`, including the former `motorway_link` ramps) form the freeway; a ramp is a surface node touched by both freeway and surface links. Off-ramp = a freeway link arrives; on-ramp = a freeway link leaves. The mainline gates that feed an off-ramp (or take an on-ramp) are the pure-source (pure-sink) freeway gates reached walking upstream (downstream) along freeway links. The Rockridge extract has 15 freeway gates (SR-24 south-west end, several carriageways; SR-24 east end toward the Caldecott Tunnel; SR-13 south-east end) and 36 ramp connections at 32 surface nodes: SR-24 at MLK Jr Way (one off, two on), 52nd St (off, on), Telegraph Ave (off at Aileen St, on at 56th St), Claremont Ave (off at Clifton St, on at Hudson St), Keith Ave, Miles Ave, Brookside Ave and Broadway (College/Broadway/Patton area), Caldecott Lane, the SR-13 terminals at Tunnel Road and Broadway Terrace, and hillside connectors at Glenwood Glade and Pinewood Road.
9. DATA + ASSUMPTION: ramp volumes. DATA: ramp ADT from [CaltransRampADT] for SR-24 and SR-13, matched to each network ramp by street name (`streetMatch`) and type (the description contains OFF or ON), or by an explicit alias where the surface street differs from the Caltrans description (`ramps.aliases`, an ASSUMPTION based on OSM destination tags, postmile order and location; Keith Ave = EB OFF TO BROADWAY, Miles Ave = WB ON FR PATTON ST and WB OFF TO COLLEGE AVE, Brookside Ave and Broadway at Brookside = EB ON FR BROADWAY, Broadway at Patton = WB OFF TO BROADWAY, Caldecott Lane = the Caldecott Ln rows). `SEG` (segment) rows count when they name exactly one street; combined rows for MLK Jr Way and 52nd St (PM R2.088, R2.100, R2.501, R2.558) are excluded because the per-street SEG rows (2012 and 2016-17) cover the same ramps and would double count. Rows of one street and type are summed and shared by the ramps of that group; ramps that alias to the same Caltrans row share it (before the fourth round each took the full row, which counted EB ON FR BROADWAY twice, 7,800 ADT). Latest count year per row; no growth applied (ASSUMPTION). The 52nd St and MLK Jr Way ramps carry much more than the four ramps of the earlier model: EB off 14,200 (52nd) and 18,400 (MLK), WB on 12,900 (52nd) and 19,300 (MLK, split over two on-ramps). Peak hour = ADT x 0.09 (ASSUMPTION, as in step 4) x period volume factor (step 5). 22 of 36 ramp connections have a published count. The other 14 (the three SR-13 terminals at Tunnel Road, Glenwood Glade and Pinewood Road, and seven Broadway connections east of Tunnel Road) use `ramps.defaultAdt` = 500 (ASSUMPTION; the SR-13/SR-24 connector rows R4.8 to R5.3 describe freeway-to-freeway movements and are not matched to surface ramps). Mainline SR-24 and SR-13 AADT (134,000 to 164,000 and 75,000 to 79,000, [CaltransAADT]) is context only: freeway-mainline-to-mainline trips never touch surface streets and are not generated.
10. Ramp demand sits at the freeway mainline gates, not at ramps. A trip that uses a ramp has the mainline gate as origin (off-ramp traffic) or destination (on-ramp traffic) and a surface gate or an in-area node as the other end; the simulator's router picks the ramp. Earlier, each flow was generated per (ramp, gate) pair, with the destination weights computed from that one ramp's bearing, which suited four ramps with one natural route each. With ramps at 52nd St, MLK Jr Way, Telegraph, Claremont, Broadway and elsewhere, a driver can choose between them, so the ramp is no longer fixed in the demand. The volume of a mainline gate is the sum of the ADT shares of the ramps it can reach (a ramp's ADT is split equally among its feeding or receiving mainline gates, so the ramp totals add up). ASSUMPTION: the choice of surface end uses direction plausibility exp(-(dTheta/90 deg)^2), floor 0.03, with dTheta the angle between the bearing ramp to destination and the bearing ramp to study-area center, averaged over the gate's ramps weighted by their volume (no distance term). Local ends take a share `ramps.localShare` = 0.60 (0.30 until the fourth round); a surface gate takes at most 40% of its target as ramp trips (`ramps.maxShareOfGate`, water-filling). Ramp trips count toward the surface gate flows, are subtracted from the residual that the Furness step (step 7) balances, and ramp trips with a local end replace part of that block's external local trips. The model's ramp volumes are consequences of routing, not inputs, so compare them with the Caltrans ramp ADT in the calibration step.
11. Car flows are cut to about `maxFlows` = 8,000 by thinning: a flow at or above a threshold t stays, a smaller flow v is kept with probability v/t (seeded) and then carries t, so the expected volume of every origin and destination is unchanged. Iterative proportional fitting on the kept pairs [DemingStephan1940] then restores each origin's and destination's volume within its class (ramp, gate to gate, local), and one factor per class restores the class total. The earlier cut kept the largest flows and renormalized with a single factor, which inflated ramp trips by 23%. With 143 surface gates, 702 local nodes and 15 mainline gates the full table has about 700,000 candidate flows per period.

Results (veh/h, weekday; surface gate-to-gate "through" / local (a gate or a block at one end, or a block to a block) / mainline-gate "ramp" trips), wider area, final settings (`assumedGateScale` 0.5, `ramps.localShare` 0.60): AM 23,720 = 9,304 / 3,309 / 11,108; midday 16,182 = 5,903 / 2,176 / 8,103; PM 24,924 = 8,572 / 3,285 / 13,068, of which 7,713 PM veh/h of ramp trips have a local end. The same table at the start of the fourth round (`assumedGateScale` 1.0, local share 0.30, before the accounting fixes): PM 28,759 = 9,827 / 1,944 / 16,988. At the freeway mainline gates in the PM, 6,703 veh/h enter the model (off-ramp trips) and 6,365 leave it (on-ramp trips). The sum of ramp ADT x K over the 36 ramps is 6,786 off and 6,282 on. Of the PM freeway-origin trips, 59% end at a block inside the area, and by compass sector from the off-ramps W takes 46%, NW 22% and SW 16%. The build log prints a gate accounting table (target and modeled inflow and outflow per gate kind, and the ratio at each counted gate) and the ramp totals; run it after every change to the demand method. The through share, the ramp share and the destination mix are consequences of the assumptions above and are uncalibrated.

## Walking and biking demand (multimodal-agents design D6)

Flows with `mode: 'pedestrian'` or `mode: 'bicycle'` are appended to each period's flow list after the car flows. Car flows have no `mode` field. The volume field is still called `vehPerHour`; for these modes it means persons per hour. Code: `tools/lib/demand-active.ts`, settings: `active` in `data/demand/rockridge.config.json`.

DATA: PM peak-hour pedestrian crosswalk volumes and bicycle movement volumes at Telegraph Ave & Alcatraz Ave in the four Oakland count reports already in `data/demand/raw/oakland-multimodal/` (mean of the four: about 151 people on foot and 82 on bicycles; the 2021 report is lower than the earlier years). Each mode gets one scaling factor so that the modeled PM peak-hour people passing that intersection on shortest paths (trip ends included) equal the observed mean. The factor applies to all periods and places. A pedestrian passing through counts as one crossing, which slightly undercounts turning trips. Nothing else is counted: the spatial pattern away from that intersection is not validated, and the model has not been calibrated against walking or biking counts.

ASSUMPTION: everything else, all labeled in the config.

1. Person trips per resident worker and per job use the car model's rates (`local.*`) and its origin and destination shares by period. Mode share: 6% walking, 4% bicycle. These only set the prior level; the count scaling then fixes the level (wider area, prior at the counted intersection, PM peak: 309 pedestrians and 776 bicycles per hour, against 151 and 82 observed, so factors of 0.49 and 0.20; the bicycle factor sits at its lower clamp, so modeled bicycles at that intersection stay above the count).
2. Pedestrians. Trips start at the LODES-based nodes (now anywhere in the model area). 20% go to or from gates within 800 m of network distance; of the rest, 35% end on a primary or secondary street inside the study area (weighted by jobs within 200 m), and the remainder go to other in-area nodes. Destination weight falls off exponentially with network distance (mean 500 m, longest walk 1,200 m). Walking legs of bus trips (5% of person trips) go between in-area nodes and bus-stop nodes within 400 m, weighted by service frequency; they need `public/data/<area>.transit.json`, so they are absent until the transit file exists. Motorway links are not walkable.
3. Bicycles. 45% of trips from in-area nodes go to or from gates (within 4 km, mean trip 2 km), the rest to other in-area nodes. Gate-to-gate through trips equal 50% of the in-area bicycle trips; a gate pair's weight is higher when its shortest path follows `bikeLane` and `bikeRoute` links (up to four times). Paths use a cost of 0.8 on bike-lane links and 0.9 on bike-route links.
4. Period profile relative to the PM peak: pedestrians 0.75 (AM), 0.9 (midday); bicycles 0.9 (AM), 0.5 (midday).
5. Flows under 0.05 persons per hour are dropped and the list is capped at 2,000 pedestrian and 1,500 bicycle flows per period (totals preserved). With cars (capped at 8,000), a period holds under 12,000 flows (PM 10,986 including 2,000 pedestrian and 1,500 bicycle flows; person volumes AM / midday / PM: pedestrians 2,388 / 2,816 / 3,075 per hour, bicycles 1,479 / 812 / 1,605 per hour). The commercial-street rule (35% of walking trips end on a primary or secondary street inside the study area) is unchanged.

Sources checked: the ACS commute mode-share tables (B08301) would give walking, bicycle and transit shares for the LODES tracts, but the Census API returned "Missing Key" without an API key and no token was available, so mode shares are assumptions. City of Berkeley and Alameda County bicycle and pedestrian counts were not searched in depth.

## Calibration changes (baseline-calibration tasks 3.x)

Full before/after table: `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md`.

- BUGFIX: a two-way street appearing as an enter-only plus an exit-only gate (same street name, within 250 m) now splits the street volume by direction instead of giving each the full volume. A truly one-way gate keeps the whole volume.
- ASSUMPTION: `classDefaultAdt` residential 800 -> 300 and tertiary 5000 -> 3000 (hillside collectors; counted Alcatraz is not representative).
- ASSUMPTION: `assumedGateScale` = 0.35 (0.3 until the second round), one factor on all gates without a count (class-default ADT). Count-based gates and ramp volumes are unscaled. After junction joining and the re-route change the network takes about 8k veh/h offered, and 0.35 is the highest value that keeps PM backlog under 2% and arterial speed above 4 m/s; 0.7 gives 124 gridlock removals. It is a stability workaround and not an estimate.
- ASSUMPTION: `ramps.localShare` 0.15 -> 0.30.
- DATA unchanged: Telegraph/Alcatraz TMC legs, Claremont override, ramp ADT.
- Second round (record steps G to J): the study area is derived from five ordered boundary streets, graph nodes of one real intersection are joined by the importer (`junctionJoinRadius`), and the simulator's re-route share is 80% per 60 s epoch. These change the network and route choice, not the demand tables; the demand was regenerated because gate and node ids moved. `ramps.localShare` stays 0.30 (0.45 and 0.6 were tested and moved the headline shares less than the seed spread).
- Third round (record steps K to O): signals, the ramp-queue rule and the checks of the re-route share and `assumedGateScale` changed the simulator, not the demand tables; `assumedGateScale` stays 0.35 and `ramps.localShare` 0.30. A direction split of ramp volumes by period (off-ramps with the inbound share, on-ramps with the outbound share) was tried and not kept: it moved Hillegass SB to freeway by 1 point and cost 9 points on Hillegass NB from freeway, and the ramp ADT has no peak-hour direction to support it.

## Wider model area (baseline-calibration task 7.1)

The model area grew from 0.016 x 0.019 degrees to 0.066 x 0.0305 degrees (design D6). What changed in the demand step:

- LODES blocks, Caltrans AADT and ramp ADT are fetched for the new bbox (`fetch-demand.ts --force`); three more Oakland intersections are read.
- Gates now sit on Ashby Ave, Sacramento St, Shattuck Ave, MLK Jr Way, Adeline St, San Pablo Ave, Tunnel Road, Broadway and the freeway mainlines; the Caltrans stations on SR-13 and SR-123 give the volumes of five of them as DATA.
- `assumedGateScale` was reset to 1.0 and `classDefaultAdt` to residential 800 and tertiary 5,000 (`ramps.defaultAdt` is 500 for the ramps without a count). The 0.35 and the 300 / 3,000 cuts had been stability workarounds for gates a few hundred meters from the study area. The fourth round (below) set `assumedGateScale` to 0.5, again as a stability workaround; the first untuned run is below.
- Ramp demand is expressed at mainline gates (step 10), and unmatched ramps no longer default to 3,000.
- Local trips start and end anywhere in the area, not only in the study area.

Untuned calibration run (`cargo run --release -p traffic-sim --bin calibrate -- --area rockridge --period pm_peak --minutes 60 --scale 1.0 --seed 1`): offered 28,759 veh/h; 9,124 trips completed, 450 gridlock removals, 4,904 trips in the origin backlog (20.4% of 24,061 spawned), arterial mean speed 3.39 m/s (2.48 in the last 15 minutes), 54.6 s wall clock. Freeway shares on Hillegass and Colby, modeled vs observed: Hillegass NB from freeway 45% vs 47%, Colby NB from freeway 48% vs 47%, Hillegass SB to freeway 62% vs 57%, Colby SB to freeway 59% vs 45% (14 points over, the only one outside the tolerance). D4 is not met; tuning of volumes, ramp demand and signal capacity follows.

Fourth round (baseline-calibration task 7.3; full table in `data/calibration/rockridge.record.md`):

- BUGFIX: one Caltrans ramp row feeding two aliased ramps was counted twice; the cut of the car flow list to 8,000 flows no longer inflates ramp and through trips (thinning plus fitting, step 11).
- ASSUMPTION: `assumedGateScale` 1.0 -> 0.5 (stability workaround, not an estimate; D4 fails at every value, including 0) and `ramps.localShare` 0.30 -> 0.60 (the model area is 5.7 by 3.4 km, so most ramp trips end inside it).
- Other accounting results: the 18 counted gates take 1.01 to 1.03 of their inflow target and 0.80 to 0.84 of their outflow target (the balancing step scales outflow to the inflow supply); the 14 ramps without a count carry 4 to 12 times their assumed 500 ADT in the simulation because the router takes the nearest exit.
- The walking and biking method is unchanged. The demand file is regenerated because node ids moved with the importer changes (`signalJoinRadius`).
