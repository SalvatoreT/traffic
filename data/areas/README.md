# Study areas

One JSON file per area, read by `tools/import-osm.ts` (`npm run data`). No area-specific values live in code.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `id`, `name` | Output file prefix and display name |
| `extract` | Overpass JSON path (`data/osm/...`) |
| `bbox` | `[minLon, minLat, maxLon, maxLat]` of the model area: ways are clipped to it and cut ends become gates. It can be much larger than the study area (Rockridge: see Model area below) |
| `studyArea` | Closed lon/lat polygon (derived from `boundary` when that is set) |
| `junctionJoinRadius` | Optional, default 20 (m): graph nodes within this distance that form one intersection are joined into one node (see Junction joining below); 0 turns it off |
| `signalJoinRadius` | Optional, default 40 (m): the join radius for clusters of two or more signal nodes (see Junction joining below) |
| `periodWindows` | Optional: hours of day `[start, end]` of each demand period (`am_peak`, `midday`, `pm_peak`). Time-conditional turn restrictions such as `no_left_turn @ (Mo-Fr 16:00-18:00)` apply in the periods whose window they overlap |
| `signalObservations` | Optional list of observed signal timings (DATA): `at` (street names that meet at the signalized junction), `approach` (street of the observed approach), `periods`, `cycleSeconds`, `greenSeconds`, `source`. The importer attaches them to the signal node as `signalTiming` with the approach's incoming links; the simulator then uses the observed cycle and green in those periods and shares the rest of the cycle among the other phases by flow (`tools/lib/signal-obs.ts`). Rockridge: Colby Street at Claremont Avenue and Forest Street, from the resident volunteer count Optional per observation: `heading` (compass direction of travel, when the street arrives from two sides and the observation is about one of them), `near` (`[lat, lon]`, when the street names match several signals; the nearest within 80 m is taken) and `ownPhase` (the approach runs in a phase of its own: observed split phasing, or a protected left turn shown as one, [FHWA-STM2008] section 4.3.4). Observations of one signal with the same periods and cycle become one timing. |
| `boundary` | Optional ordered list of boundary streets; `npx tsx tools/lib/derive-study-area.ts data/areas/<id>.json` rewrites `studyArea` from it. An entry is a street name (matched against OSM `name` or `ref`) or an object `{name?, ref?, class?}` (for example `{"ref": "CA 24", "class": "motorway"}`). Consecutive entries, and the last with the first, meet at the geometric crossing of their centerlines: a shared node, or a grade-separated crossing; with several crossings (a dual carriageway) the one nearest the area center is used, which puts the edge on the inner carriageway. Each edge follows the way geometry vertex by vertex (surface edges are simplified to 3 m; freeway edges keep every vertex). There is no outward buffer: links lying on a boundary street (center within 8 m of the polygon edge) count as inside, while motorway-class links never do. Rockridge: Telegraph Ave, Alcatraz Ave, College Ave, Claremont Ave, SR-24 (a five-sided area; nothing south-east of Claremont) |
| `approachGroups` | Optional (round 21): named sets of outside nodes that say where a through car comes from or is going, in a resident's words. `groups` is an ordered list of rules `{id, label, match}`; `match` may hold `names` (a link at the gate has one of these street names), `classes`, `role` (`entry`: trips can only enter the model there, `exit`: only leave) and `sector` (`[from, to]`, compass degrees clockwise from north, measured from the study area's center to the gate, wrapping through 360). The first rule that matches a gate takes it; a gate no rule matches stops the demand build. `overrides` maps a node id to a group id for gates the rules place wrongly. `sets` names unions of groups. A node that is not a gate takes the group of the nearest surface gate (ASSUMPTION). The reserved id `study-area` stands for the nodes inside the study area. The demand build writes the result to `data/demand/<id>.groups.json` (`tools/lib/approach-groups.ts`); the calibrate report and the `intents` of assumed movement flows use it. Rockridge: 20 groups for 123 gates (Telegraph Ave and College Ave from the north, Alcatraz Ave from the west, the SR-24 on-ramps and off-ramps by direction, and the streets of each compass side) |
| `center`, `zoom` | Map view for `public/data/areas.json` |

## Import defaults

Classes: `motorway`/`motorway_link` become motorway; `primary`, `secondary`, `tertiary` (+ `_link`) keep their class;
`residential`, `unclassified`, `living_street` become residential; `service` stays service (skipped when `service=parking_aisle|driveway|drive-through` [OSMService]
or `access`/`motor_vehicle` is `private`/`no`). Footways, cycleways, paths, steps and pedestrian ways appear only in the basemap.

| Class | Speed when `maxspeed` missing | Lanes per direction when `lanes` missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| motorway | 65 mph (ramps: 35 mph) | 2 |
| primary | 30 mph | 2 |
| secondary | 30 mph | 1 |
| tertiary | 25 mph | 1 |
| residential | 25 mph (California prima facie) | 1 |
| service | 15 mph | 1 |

Lanes: `lanes:forward`/`lanes:backward`, else `lanes` (one-way: all; two-way: floor(lanes/2), minimum 1). Unitless `maxspeed` is km/h per the OSM spec.

Direction: `oneway=yes|-1`, roundabouts and motorways (unless `oneway=no`) are one-way; a two-way segment yields two linked links.

Control: `highway=traffic_signals` on a junction or on an approach within 30 m is a signal. `highway=stop` is attributed to the
nearest junction along its way (within 60 m) and, with `direction=forward|backward|both`, to the approach heading into it;
`stop=all` forces all-way stop. Roundabout rings (collapsed to one node) and `mini_roundabout` become a traffic circle. Stops on all approaches make an all-way stop; stops on some make a two-way stop.

Gates: ends of ways clipped by `bbox`, plus dead ends within 60 m of the bbox edge. A gate should sit mid-block on a corridor that leaves the model, not inside a junction: the importer prints a warning listing every gate within `gateSignalWarnM` (default 60 m) of a signalized junction, and `tools/import-osm.test.ts` checks that list and that at most 3% of gates are on it. Choose the `bbox` edges by running the importer for a few candidate values (the extract may be larger than the `bbox`). Only the largest strongly connected
component (plus one-way spurs leading to or from gates) is kept. Staggered T-junctions: two T-junctions on an undivided street are one intersection when their side-street legs carry the same name, leave on opposite sides of the through street, and the junction points lie within `staggeredJoinRadius` (default 35 m, 0 turns it off) along it, whatever the control. This is separate from, and narrower than, the general join radius above: it is keyed on the cross street's name, so the same offset with two different side-street names, or the same name at 60 m, stays two junctions. Rockridge: Colby St/62nd St (29 m, now one node with two Colby St and two 62nd St legs, like Hillegass Ave/62nd St) and 13 more pairs in the model, 5 of them in the study area (Alcatraz Ave/Colby St, 62nd St/Colby St, Ayala Ave/Martin St, 60th St/Howell St, Howell St/McAuley St); the others are 411 m or more away. Result: 1,781 nodes, 4,450 links, study area 69 nodes and 197 links (1,795, 4,478, 74 and 207 before). Freeway-edge stubs: the box can cut one freeway carriageway into several stubs a few meters apart, each with its own gate (a carriageway that forks just outside the box). Mainline gates of one direction (all links freeway class, similar bearing, stub ends within `freewayGateMergeM`, default 80 m, by single linkage) are merged into one gate at their centroid; parallel stub links between the same two nodes become one link with the lanes added; a fork node that was a gate only because its other branch was clipped stops being one. The importer lists each merge and checks that every on-ramp reaches a mainline exit gate and every off-ramp is reachable from a mainline entry gate (a freeway-class link between two surface nodes is a connector, not a ramp); violations are warned and listed. Rockridge: the SR-24 west end had three eastbound entry stubs (two 2-lane, one 3-lane) and three westbound exit stubs, the east end two entry and two exit stubs; they are now one gate per direction and end, so the 52nd St and Martin Luther King Jr Way ramps share the west-end gate with the Claremont/Hudson and Miles Ave ramps (125 gates). Humps: `traffic_calming=hump|bump|table|cushion` on a way interior.

Study-area flags: motorway-class links and nodes touched only by motorway links are never `inStudyArea` (the freeway and its ramps are not local streets), so cut-through and safety metrics ignore them.

Junction joining: graph nodes within `junctionJoinRadius` (default 20 m) of their cluster centroid that are connected by a short non-freeway link, and share a street name (or are both signals), become one node at the centroid (dual carriageways, offset signal tags, offset T junctions, ramp terminals). Clusters of two or more signal nodes may reach `signalJoinRadius` (default 40 m), because offset signals on a wide junction and five-leg crossings such as Shattuck/Adeline/Ward are one intersection. A cluster is joined only if its internal links make it strongly connected, so no impossible movement appears; it never contains a gate, a node on the extract edge (within 2 m), a traffic circle or a freeway link. Control: signal if any member is a signal; all-way stop if every remaining approach has a stop; otherwise a two-way stop if some approach has one. Internal links are dropped, approaches keep their `stopAtEnd` flags and humps.

Staggered intersections whose legs meet within the join radius (Hillegass Ave / 62nd St) are treated as one intersection on purpose.

Signal pairs that stay separate: one-way chains, where a one-way link joins the two signals (Broadway at Keith Ave and Patton St, the frontage ramps there, Ashby Ave at Adeline St, parts of Shattuck/Adeline/Ward), and pairs 41 to 59 m apart (63rd St/San Pablo Ave at 47 m, MLK Jr Way/Woolsey St at 41 m, Tunnel Rd/Hiller Dr at 46 m). Joining a one-way chain would allow movements from the downstream member to the upstream member that do not exist. The pairs at the south edge (West St/40th St, Harlan St/40th St) are joined now.

Turn restrictions: OSM `restriction` relations with a `from` way, a single `via` node and a `to` way become `turnBans` entries (`from` link into the junction, `to` link out of it) that the simulator applies to cars, not to bicycles [OSMRestriction]. `no_*` bans that pair; `only_*` bans every other movement from the `from` link except the U-turn. `no_u_turn` is skipped because the simulator already forbids U-turns away from dead ends. Skipped: `via` ways and several `via` members, relations with `except` naming motorcars, and types other than left, right and straight-on. A `restriction:conditional` relation (for example Ashby Ave to Hillegass Ave, no left or right turn Mo-Fr 16:00-18:00) becomes a ban listing the demand periods it overlaps, `periods: ["pm_peak"]`; the simulator drops bans of other periods when it starts. Rockridge: 199 restriction relations in the extract; they gave 69 always-on and 11 timed `turnBans` entries (an `only_*` relation gives several). 91 relations were skipped (mostly `no_u_turn`) and 34 matched no link because the `via` node is not a junction of the network. `npx tsx tools/fetch-osm.ts --restrictions` adds the relations to an existing extract (map API tiles, because the Overpass mirrors do not answer).

Left-turn lanes: `leftLanes` on a link counts the lanes tagged left-turn only in `turn:lanes` (`turn:lanes:forward` or `:backward` on two-way ways) at the end of the way [OSMTurnLanes]. OSM lane counts already include that lane, so the simulator's left-turn pocket takes one of the existing lanes where the tag is present and adds a lane only where it is absent (class rule). Rockridge: 185 links, 112 of them at signals.

Collapsed circles: each `junction=roundabout|circular` ring becomes one `traffic_circle` node at the ring centroid (ring ways are dropped, approaches end at the center). Gate nodes carry `canEnter`/`canExit` flags (an outgoing / incoming link connected to the main network); demand uses only enterable origins and exitable destinations.

## Model area and study area

The network, basemap and demand cover the `bbox`; the `studyArea` polygon (derived from `boundary`) marks the part the metrics and the cut-through definition refer to. Everything outside it is context that supplies realistic route choice and traffic at the study-area edge. Changing the `bbox` changes the projection origin (the bbox center) and so every node and link id and every x, y value, but not the study-area outline or the set of in-study streets.

Rockridge `bbox` is `[-122.2845, 37.8325, -122.220, 37.8605]` (about 5.6 km by 3.1 km). The first wider model (fourth round) used `[-122.286, 37.830, -122.220, 37.8605]`; the sixth round moved the west and south edges inward so that gates stop falling inside junctions (see the table and the gate check). The edges contain these streets and ramps:

| Edge | Value | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| North | 37.8605 | Ashby Ave (SR-13) reaches 37.8581 at Tunnel Road; the rest is margin |
| West | -122.2845 | Sacramento St at -122.282 to -122.278, plus margin. Ashby Ave is cut between San Pablo Ave and Sacramento St. At -122.286 the edge ran through San Pablo Ave and the Ashby/San Pablo signal (gates 0 to 6 m from it) |
| South | 37.8325 | 51st and 52nd St, and the SR-24 ramps at 51st/52nd St, Telegraph and Shattuck/MLK, all lie north of it (the MLK ramps reach 37.8335). 40th St (37.8257 to 37.8314) lies wholly south of it; at 37.830 the edge sliced 40th St and its signals at West St, San Pablo Ave, Adeline St and Market St, and nine gates sat within 60 m of a signal. Extending the edge south instead would add the I-980/I-580/SR-24 interchange (MacArthur Maze) and freeway ramps with no count, so the edge moved north. Candidates from 37.8325 to 37.8395 left 1 to 10 gates near signals, and 37.8325 left 1 |
| East | -122.220 | Tunnel Road with the Caldecott Lane ramps (-122.2224 to -122.2213) and the SR-24/SR-13 interchange (-122.2296 to -122.2277); SR-13 leaves the box cleanly in the south-east |

Import result at this size (`npx tsx tools/import-osm.ts`): 1,800 nodes and 4,480 links (residential 2,708, tertiary 681, secondary 402, service 343, primary 247, motorway 99). Controls: 94 signals, 85 all-way stops, 424 two-way stops, 37 traffic circles, 1,160 uncontrolled. 132 gates: 119 on surface streets, 13 on freeway mainlines (SR-24 and SR-13, several carriageways each). The same 36 ramp connections join SR-24 and SR-13 to the surface network. The earlier box (2,162 nodes, 5,401 links, 158 gates, 112 signals) had 17 gates within 60 m of a signal (9 south, 8 west); this one has 2 (Ashby Ave west, 22 m from the Ashby/Sacramento signal, and Broadway south, 11 m). Both lie on counted corridors and stay. The study area keeps 74 nodes and 207 links, the same streets as the narrower extract. Lane counts of 1,142 links on Ashby Ave, Shattuck Ave, Adeline St, MLK Jr Way, Sacramento St, 51st St, Broadway, Tunnel Rd, 40th St, San Pablo Ave, Market St, West St, Telegraph Ave, College Ave, Claremont Ave, Alcatraz Ave, 52nd St and Pleasant Valley Ave were compared with the OSM `lanes`, `lanes:forward` and `lanes:backward` tags of their ways (fourth round): none differs.

Edge effects: nodes within 2 m of the bbox edge are never joined, and a gate is never joined. In the first wider box the West St/40th St intersection lay 6 to 17 m inside the south edge, so its gates sat a few meters from the signal and the intersection had no storage for gate traffic; that corner and the other south-edge signals (40th St at San Pablo Ave, Adeline St and Market St) were among the largest sources of gridlock removals. They are outside the current box. The demand builder drops LODES blocks outside the `bbox` (the raw file was cut for the larger box), so their local trips no longer exist.
